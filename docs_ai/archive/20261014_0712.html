Can Language Models Play the Wikipedia Game?
Authors: Alexander Hickey, Joanna Knight, James Bishop
First: 2026-10-09T17:59:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:56+00:00
Comments: 64 pages (14-page main text, references and 16 appendices), 17 figures, 10 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language models increasingly support or automate information retrieval from document collections too large to index. This task is difficult when it requires specialist linguistic or scientific knowledge. We used the Wikipedia Game to test language models on an analogous information retrieval task. In the game, players must reach a target article using only article links. We compared sentence embedding models and LLMs across five Wikipedia languages and five academic-literature datasets. We construct very large local graphs of Wikipedia pages and of paper citations using OpenAlex and provide code to build and navigate them. This allows the study of language models' navigation capabilities without relying on third-party APIs. We compared the models with human and TF-IDF benchmarks. The Wikipedia Game format enables robust comparisons across techniques, languages, and domains. No model significantly outperformed humans, although the best models (GPT-o3 mini, GPT-4o, QwQ:32B, and DeepSeek-R1:70B) achieved comparable success. Performance and rankings varied across languages and topics, with implications for practitioners utilising these techniques.

Measuring Harness-Induced Belief Divergence in Multi-Step LLM Agents
Authors: Haiwen Yi, Xinyuan Song
First: 2026-07-05T22:22:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:01+00:00
Comments: 15 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Software agents are becoming increasingly important in modern coding workflows, where they solve complex tasks through multi-step interaction with code repositories. A central component of these systems is the execution harness, which governs how the underlying LLM observes, acts on, and verifies its environment. We show that the harness can in turn shape the agent's evolving beliefs about the environment, even when the task, environment, and base LLM remain fixed. To reveal this effect, we introduce a belief-rollout diagnostic that traces how an agent's anticipated world state and future decisions evolve under alternative harnesses. We further separate immediate divergence introduced by the interface from divergence that develops over longer horizons. Across controlled coding tasks include HIBench-Code, SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, we find substantial cross-harness belief divergence that is often invisible from terminal success alone and can propagate into different downstream actions. Building on this observation, we introduce \textbf{Belief-Invariant World-Modeling (BIWM)}, which exposes evidence hidden or compressed by harness execution and aligns the resulting belief trajectories across different harnesses. Our findings reveal that harness design can systematically shape how software agents understand and act on their environments, providing practical guidance for building and evaluating more reliable coding agents.

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback
Authors: Yucheng Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Shusheng Yang, Jiakang Chen, Serena Yeung-Levy
First: 2026-10-09T17:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:58:15+00:00

Abstract
Multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) allow the evolving image to update internal text representations through joint attention. These representations then guide subsequent image updates, raising a question: does this feedback help correct deviations from the prompt, or preserve them? We find that image-to-text (I2T) feedback can preserve the objects and attributes already represented in the evolving image, including errors. Transplanting I2T messages can rescue failures or disrupt successes. At matched intervention strengths, suppressing the influence of I2T feedback improves generation, whereas opposite controls degrade it. These findings motivate Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), which suppresses the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens this feedback and weakens text-to-image conditioning. The in-place variant, iSFG, directly suppresses I2T feedback and strengthens text-to-image conditioning, requiring no extra model evaluations. Temporal interventions motivate running the SFG branch during the first 20% of image sampling steps, requiring about $1.2\times$ the base NFE without classifier-free guidance (CFG). Experiments across three image-generation backbones show gains in alignment and preference-model scores. On FLUX, adding SFG to CFG raises GenEval from $0.6661$ to $0.7479$; on SD3.5M, iSFG adds $+1.33$ HPSv2.1 over CFG at zero extra NFE. The control also transfers to text-to-video and image-to-video generation, improving VBench scores. Code is available at https://github.com/yyc1215lll-creator/SFG.

Evolving Hybrid Quantum-Classical Architectures for Image Classification
Authors: Devroop Kar, Daniel Krutz, Travis Desell

Abstract
Hybrid quantum classical neural networks integrate parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with established deep learning architectures, but their performance depends strongly on the choice of quantum circuit architecture, a choice that remains largely manual. Most existing approaches rely on hand-designed or fixed circuit ansätze, requiring circuit structure, gate composition, and qubit connectivity to be specified in advance with no guarantee that they suit the task. This limitation is especially acute in image classification, where quantum circuits must transform features extracted by classical networks while remaining compact enough for practical training, requirements that generic, task-agnostic ansätze are unlikely to satisfy simultaneously. We extend EXAQC, an evolutionary framework for automated quantum circuit discovery, to image classification. EXAQC evolves PQCs as intermediate processing modules while retaining classical feature-extraction and prediction layers. On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, EXAQC achieves 98.42%, 90.62%, and 85.47% accuracy, respectively, while using comparable gate counts to other quantum architecture-search methods. Against classical networks, evolved hybrid models maintain comparable accuracy with substantially fewer trainable parameters, reaching 85.68% on CIFAR-10 with over 25$\times$ fewer parameters than a 10-layer CNN. Encoding choice also matters: rotation-based encodings (RX, RY, U3) outperform amplitude encoding by 22-25 points on CIFAR-10. These results demonstrate that automated circuit discovery yields compact quantum modules that can replace larger classical components in vision architectures while retaining competitive accuracy.

Abstract
Recent Audio Multimodal Large Language Models (Audio MLLMs) demonstrate impressive performance on speech benchmarks, yet it remains unclear whether these models genuinely process acoustic signals or rely on text-based semantic inference. To systematically study this question, we introduce DEAF (Diagnostic Evaluation of Acoustic Faithfulness), a benchmark of over 2,700 conflict stimuli spanning three acoustic dimensions: emotional prosody, background sounds, and speaker identity. Then, we design a controlled multi-level evaluation framework that progressively increases textual influence, ranging from semantic conflicts in the content to misleading prompts and their combination, allowing us to disentangle content-driven bias from prompt-induced sycophancy. Our evaluation of seven Audio MLLMs reveals a consistent pattern of text dominance: models are sensitive to acoustic variations, yet predictions are predominantly driven by textual inputs, revealing a gap between high performance on standard speech benchmarks and genuine acoustic understanding.

Authors: Kosio Beshkov

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The expressivity of a neural network $Φ$ describes the class of functions that a network can express. The term topological expressivity refers to a family of measures particularly suited to understand binary classification and is often formulated in terms of the Betti numbers of a decision boundary $β(Φ^{-1}(0))$. Despite its popularity, this measure is not as useful for regression and other problems that do not involve a decision boundary. Instead, we propose to study topological expressivity in terms of the Betti numbers of the output of a neural network $β(Φ(X))$. We formally prove that a ReLU network with architecture $(d,\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d } β_k(3k+2),\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}β_k(k+1))$ can express spaces homotopy equivalent to wedge sums of spheres with arbitrary Betti numbers. This gives upper bounds for any arbitrary connected ($β_0=1$) sequence of Betti numbers, but by constructing spaces homotopy equivalent to products of $N$ spheres of dimension $d_1,...,d_N$ we achieve much better bounds $(\sum\limits_{n=1} d_n, 3\sum\limits_{n=1}\limits^N d_n + 2N, \sum\limits_{n=1}\limits^N d_n + N) $ for Betti numbers given by $\prod_n (1+x^{d_n})$. Finally we conjecture a much stronger tight bound which gives the architectural bound $(d,d+1,d+1)$ for a space homeomorphic to the sphere.

Chronos Policy: Learning Robot Policies on Physical Time
Authors: Changhao Wang, Jaden Clark, Shuran Song
First: 2026-10-09T17:55:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:55:20+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://changhaowang.github.io/chronospolicy/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robot policies often convert sensing and control into fixed-rate observation-action sequences, leaving physical time implicit in sequence position. This assumption breaks when observation rates or delays vary, or when inference latency changes. We introduce Chronos Policy, which makes physical time explicit by conditioning on observation capture times and desired action execution times. We study two ways to incorporate time into a diffusion transformer: adding timestamp embeddings to token features, or encoding physical time in positional encoding through time-aware rotary positional encodings (time-aware RoPE). We find that time-aware RoPE is more robust, allowing attention to depend directly on physical time while keeping token content unchanged. During training, we temporally randomize which observations are sampled and when the action chunk begins, exposing the policy to a wider range of sensing and execution schedules that may occur during deployments. This also enables learning from asynchronous, multi-rate data without forcing all measurements onto a fixed temporal grid. Across simulation and real-world experiments, Chronos Policy improves performance on dynamic tasks, where observation-to-action timing is critical, and on multisensory tasks, where the relative timing between observations matters. On standard quasi-static tasks, it preserves nominal performance while improving robustness to changes in sensing and inference timing.

Abstract
Capability evaluations aim to surface dangerous model behaviors before deployment, but their reliability depends on the assumption that hidden capabilities can be elicited. We show this assumption does not hold under adversarial conditions by constructing backdoor attacks that embed encrypted neural networks within host model weights, decrypting and executing them only upon receiving a secret trigger. Using digital lockers, these hidden circuits remain provably hard to elicit and interpret under cryptographic assumptions, even given full access to model weights, extending prior work from hiding fixed strings to arbitrary computations. We implement our proof of concept constructions in PyTorch and evaluate elicitation through fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient-based trigger search, and latent adversarial training on a small chemical reaction prediction task - a proxy for CBRN-relevant capabilities. Our results demonstrate resistance to these methods and, together with our cryptographic guarantees, reveal a limit of elicitation-based capability audits. We release our models to support the improvement of defenses.

Abstract
Modern machines can solve equations, follow logic and reason through language, yet they still struggle with problems that require visual non-verbal reasoning. This is evident even in advanced systems such as DeepSeek, ChatGPT and Gemini, which remain limited on visual tasks such as completing abstract patterns or delineating ambiguous regions-of-interest. Unlike explicit intermediate steps in verbal reasoning which can be verified through logic or automated tests, visual reasoning involves implicit intermediate steps with no clear methods for verification. Non-verbal reasoning is therefore challenging to model and, as a result, remains a fundamental limitation of current machine intelligence. Here we introduce GenVerOpt, a general framework that unifies generation, verification and optimisation into a reasoning pipeline for machines. By generating candidate solutions, verifying their correctness, and iteratively refining them, GenVerOpt enables per-sample optimisation rather than relying solely on dataset-level trends captured during model training. This process mirrors human problem-solving where multiple possibilities are imagined, evaluated, and refined until a correct solution emerges. Across diverse domains, GenVerOpt demonstrates capabilities beyond existing systems: it surpasses humans on abstract IQ puzzles, matches clinicians in prostate cancer segmentation, exceeds experts in liver vessel reconstruction and outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in tumour boundary alignment. These results show that machine reasoning can extend beyond language, establishing visual non-verbal reasoning as a foundation for more flexible and generalisable intelligence.

Abstract
Deep-space communications operate under intermittent connectivity, long propagation delays, and relay storage constraints. Abstract
While deep learning models for tabular data have recently received significant research attention, the choice of objective functions for regression tasks remains an understudied degree of freedom. Several lines of research offer alternatives to the standard Mean Squared Error (MSE), but differences in datasets, backbones, and evaluation protocols make their practical benefits difficult to compare.
