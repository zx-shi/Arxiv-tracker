AtomicRL: Atomic Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation
Authors: Likui Zhang, Zhe Zhu, Zhihao Zhan, Yibo Bai, Hanlin Wang, Dongwei Li, Bin Wang, Tianle Zhang, Xiaodan Liang, Liang Lin
First: 2026-10-21T17:45:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:45:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown promise in robotic manipulation, but imitation learning (IL) alone often struggles to master complex long-horizon skills. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers an effective avenue for further improving VLA policies. However, existing VLA-RL methods typically suffer from delayed and biased credit assignment due to trajectory-level sparse rewards, while imbalanced subtask mastery creates bottlenecks that limit overall performance. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework that leverages atomic task structures for reliable credit assignment and adaptive exploration. AtomicRL introduces Semantic-Aware Policy Optimization (SAPO), which uses semantic-aware gating to constrain TD residuals from failed atomic subtasks from propagating across task boundaries, mitigating credit contamination while preserving intra-subtask credit assignment and value bootstrapping. In addition, Bottleneck-Adaptive Target Exploration (BATE) dynamically coordinates task sampling and local exploration incentives based on the mastery of individual atomic subtasks, directing learning toward the remaining bottlenecks. Finally, we develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that bridges online RL in simulation with real-world deployment, facilitating practical transfer to physical robots. On LIBERO-LONG, AtomicRL achieves absolute performance gains of 3.6% and 4.0% over PPO across two VLA architectures. On CALVIN ABC-D, it improves full-task performance by up to 9.5% over PPO. Combined with the Real2Sim2Real pipeline, AtomicRL further improves real-world task performance by an average of 21.3%. These results demonstrate that AtomicRL effectively improves the learning efficiency and manipulation performance of VLA policies on long-horizon tasks.

Abstract
Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effective interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single action representation for both task intent and whole-body coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms. SP-VLA, a vision-language-action model, first predicts task-relevant sparse poses and finger joint positions from multimodal observations and language instructions using asynchronous action-chunk inference. Given these sparse targets, SP-MGM, a motion generation model trained through two-stage distillation, reconstructs coordinated full-body motion in real time. A pretrained motion encoder maps this motion to a compact latent representation. SP-GMT, a general motion tracker, then integrates the explicit sparse targets with this implicit motion prior for whole-body control. Experiments in simulation and on a real humanoid robot demonstrate improved motion tracking, whole-body coordination, and loco-manipulation success over existing approaches. Additional results and videos are available on our \href{https://sp-hlm.github.io/}{\textcolor{blue}{project page}}.

Abstract
A patient who has lost part of the skull vault needs an implant that fits the defect exactly, and such implants are still drawn by hand in CAD by specialists, which makes each case slow and expensive. The task can be considered as a shape completion problem, so pretrained completion models, and now the first shape completion foundation models, look like a way to remove that manual step. We audited three publicly released pretrained shape completion models, two of them foundation models, applied zero-shot to 239 cranial defects, some carved from public skull datasets and some real post-craniectomy cases. Each output was compared with simply returning the patient's own remaining bone, and tested for whether the predicted material could be placed without intersecting the bone that is still there. On the 210 carved defects none of the three improved on doing nothing, and on the 29 real cases only one did so beyond chance. Yet not one output, from any model in any configuration, was implantable: none managed both to stay clear of the bone the patient still has and to place more material than doing nothing, where a task-specific volumetric model, trained for this task and selected on these cases, was implantable on all but one. The output that most convincingly resembles a skull is no more placeable than the rest, and at these point densities implant thickness cannot be read off the emitted points at all, which is a property of the output representation rather than of any one model. On this evidence the shape completion foundation models we could obtain cannot be trusted and, used as released, are not usable in clinical practice. Substantial research towards trustworthy foundation models for shape completion is still needed.

Privacy Accounting for Empirical Denoisers

Abstract
Diffusion models have emerged as the foundation of modern generative systems, yet their high memorization capacity raises privacy concerns. While differentially private (DP) training provides formal guarantees, it comes at a cost in sample quality. In this work, we study a complementary question: how much privacy loss is incurred during the sampling process itself? Directly analyzing modern neural denoisers is challenging because their sensitivity to a single training example is generally intractable. To overcome this challenge, we construct and analyze a clipped privacy-accountable empirical denoiser, a training-free estimator of the Bayes-optimal denoiser, whose per-step sensitivity can be computed explicitly, allowing each denoising step to be a Gaussian mechanism. This perspective enables privacy accounting under Gaussian DP when each training example contributes to each denoising step, and under $f$-DP trade-off functions when each generated sample uses a single randomly subsampled block of the training data. We report every $(\varepsilon, δ)$ guarantee as a certified upper bound from a privacy loss distribution accountant, and use a cumulant-based Edgeworth approximation as a fast diagnostic, characterizing where it is reliable and where it becomes anti-conservative. We also analyze the privacy loss along the denoising trajectory and show that it grows steeply near the critical window in which semantic content is formed. This observation motivates hybrid samplers that use the private empirical denoiser through the formative steps where it remains a faithful proxy for the neural denoiser, then switch to a public neural denoiser for the remaining refinement. Our results on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-64 show that the privacy of diffusion models depends not only on the training procedure, but also on sampling-time design choices such as subsampling, stopping time, and hybrid denoising.

Abstract
Understanding the internal mechanisms of deep neural networks remains a central challenge in machine learning. In computer vision, one promising yet only preliminarily explored approach is feature inversion via inverse networks, which reconstructs images from intermediate representations using trained inverse networks. In this study, we revisit feature inversion via inverse networks, introducing a novel, modular variant that enables a computationally more efficient application of the technique while, in some cases, producing semantically more coherent image reconstructions. ClunoGS: Compact Language 3D Gaussian Splatting via Unified Optimization
Authors: Jian Xu, Yi Li, Ying Huang, Siyi Wu, Bingzhe Li, Yuede Ji, Miao Yin

Abstract
Language 3D Gaussian Splatting enables open-vocabulary scene understanding by associating Gaussian primitives with language features. These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially. However, pruning based only on rendering quality can remove Gaussians that have small RGB contributions but are important for semantic prediction. Once removed, their semantic information cannot be recovered by later quantization. We propose ClunoGS, a compact framework for Language 3DGS that combines semantics-aware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a unified constrained objective. Semantic supervision is used during sparsification, and the objective jointly considers rendering quality, semantic accuracy, and compactness. We optimize the unified objective with alternating primal, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates. We evaluate ClunoGS on open-vocabulary localization and segmentation using the LERF and ScanNet-10 benchmarks. Under aggressive compression, our unified, semantics-aware formulation substantially alleviates the degradation of semantic representations caused by sequential compression and semantics-agnostic Gaussian sparsification. Ablations further show that jointly coordinating sparsification and quantization better preserves semantic accuracy than applying them independently.

Abstract
Recovering metric world-space hand motion from egocentric video supports understanding human interactions and transferring them to embodied agents. Three challenges complicate this task: multi-stage pipelines propagate errors from independently estimated hand and camera motion; monocular observations leave physical scale ambiguous; and occlusion and out-of-view hands admit multiple plausible motion trajectories. We present DewHand, a unified model for direct joint reconstruction of world-space hand and camera motion. To avoid compounding errors from multi-stage estimation, DewHand jointly predicts hand and camera motion in a shared world coordinate system within a single-stage pipeline. To resolve metric-scale ambiguity, it combines scene-level geometric features from VGGT-$Ω$ with metric-space supervision, while hand-specific features preserve fine-grained articulation. To handle occlusion and out-of-view hands, we formulate reconstruction as conditional motion generation with flow matching. Under the same architecture, this generative formulation outperforms direct regression in both accuracy and temporal smoothness with only two sampling steps. Experiments on HOT3D and ARCTIC demonstrate improved world-space accuracy and temporal consistency over state-of-the-art methods. DewHand reduces rigid-aligned world-space error relative to StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm on HOT3D and from 76.2 to 36.2 mm on ARCTIC. It also recovers camera scale within 10% on two thirds of HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM. Moreover, in zero-shot evaluations on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, captured with different devices, DewHand achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space error (WA-MPJPE) on each dataset.

Abstract

Abstract
Geometric structure is essential for robotic manipulation and understanding the 3D world. Most VLA models rely on 2D representations of current observations, while geometry-aware approaches often require additional 3D information. However, geometry inferred independently from individual observations is often discarded rather than accumulated into a persistent task representation. Our key insight is that action generation needs a compact, task-relevant geometric abstraction that can be continuously updated, rather than a complete, renderable 3D reconstruction at every step. We introduce GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that internalizes explicit 3D supervision into dedicated geometric tokens and maintains them as temporal memory for action generation, without directly aligning the VLM's native visual embeddings with the teacher feature space. During training, a frozen geometry foundation model supervises learnable GeoPlan tokens through a geometric flow-matching objective over the current scene point map and future end-effector tracks. After benchmark-specific downstream adaptation, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average success on LIBERO and 91.10% / 91.24% on RoboTwin's clean/randomized settings. Across five real-world tasks, GeoMem-VLA achieves 78.0% average success. These results suggest that internalizing and remembering 3D scene understanding is a promising approach to robot control.

Abstract

Abstract
General-purpose robots must handle tasks, objects and environments they were never trained on, yet zero-shot generalization remains one of the least solved problems in physical AI. In digital AI, zero-shot capability grew with the volume and, above all, the diversity of training data, much of it sourced from the crowd. Robotics instead relies on paid collection by a few well-funded teams, which yields volume without diversity. We formulate zero-shot generalization in physical AI as coverage of the deployment distribution, and show that more samples from the same collectors shrink the unseen region only slowly, while each new contributor can extend it. As a scalable source of diverse data, we propose a community of developers building applications on one standard, approachable robot, Origami. We close with a testable hypothesis and an evaluation protocol relating real-robot zero-shot success to the diversity of contributed data.

Abstract
Incorporating AI systems into safety systems requires monitoring to avoid silent faults and ensure safe and reliable operation. Single monitors often do not provide actionable information for human supervisors, who act as the system's adaptive controller. We present COMET, a coordinated multi-layer runtime monitoring framework for human-supervised AI-enabled safety systems in which each monitor is anchored in a different fault mode. Monitor outputs are combined in a coordination layer and mapped by a communication layer to a traffic-light display. In this work, COMET consists of three monitoring layers: signal integrity, physics consistency, and the AI system's competence envelope. The framework is evaluated on an open dataset of compressed-air leaks, where an AI system detects leaks from acoustic emissions, with held-out conditions simulating covariate shift, semantic shift, and sensor faults, events expected during runtime but unknown in their exact appearance. All monitors are frozen after development and tested zero-shot, and the framework is compared against no-monitoring and single-monitor setups. The bare AI system fails silently and confidently under all three shift types, whereas COMET reduces the trusted-wrong-answer rate to zero on covariate-shifted no-leak data and on three of four sensor faults, at a nominal false-alarm rate of $\approx 1\%$. Single monitors come close in detection but cannot attribute the fault mode. COMET converts their anonymous detection flags into diagnoses. Residual silent misses under semantic shift define the boundary of passive monitoring. Runtime monitoring frameworks for AI-enabled safety systems must therefore combine quantitative fault detection and isolation with qualitative, human-oriented feedback.

Abstract
We present FoveaVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that replaces the wrist camera with learned foveation actions. Wrist cameras provide close-up detail but restrict the view to the end-effector's surroundings. External cameras see the whole scene, but the policy receives them at low resolution, so the fine detail needed for manipulation is lost. FoveaVLA recovers this detail by predicting, alongside each action chunk, the interaction region to observe at the next policy query. The policy then receives crops of these regions, taken from the original-resolution images, together with the full views. We build FoveaVLA on $π_{0.5}$ in two configurations, Single and Dual, using one or two external stereo cameras with full and foveal RGB and depth inputs, and post-train both on DROID with RoboInter interaction annotations. Across four real-world task suites, FoveaVLA-Single achieves 70.8% success with one external RGB-D view, and FoveaVLA-Dual achieves 77.1%, versus 52.1% for the wrist-equipped $π_{0.5}$. Both match the baseline on plain pick-and-place and improve success with distractors, cloth manipulation, and far-away objects.

Abstract

Abstract
Object detection models rely on large-scale annotated datasets that require substantial manual annotation effort. The labeling costs further increase when annotation uncertainty is captured through soft labels, as multiple annotations must be collected for each object. We address this challenge by introducing a semi-supervised framework for the uncertainty-aware annotation of object detection datasets. Our approach leverages open-vocabulary object detectors to generate region proposals and semantically meaningful object representations. Based on these representations, we construct an object-level similarity graph and propagate probabilistic label information. Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies need to recognize gaps in their training knowledge to request useful demonstrations or assistance in new situations, especially when failures are costly. Disagreement within an ensemble of independently trained policies can guide these decisions, but requires training and running multiple policies. We propose post-hoc kernel-variance heads that estimate epistemic uncertainty from the embeddings of a single policy. Each head computes the conditional variance of a Gaussian process conditioned on embeddings from the training demonstrations. Fitting requires no regression targets or gradient updates and leaves the policy unchanged. In experiments with SmolVLA on LIBERO-10, the kernel variance heads perform competitively with two-policy ensemble scores in task-level correlation with task success, in demonstration acquisition during active learning, and in referral to a stronger supervisor policy as a proxy for human assistance.
