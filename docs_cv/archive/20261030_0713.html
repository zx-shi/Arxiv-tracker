ThinkWorld: World Imagination Reinforces Chain-of-Dynamics Reasoning
Authors: Chaehyun Kim, Chi-Pin Huang, Jisoo Kim, Min-Hung Chen, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang, Seungryong Kim, Fu-En Yang
First: 2026-10-27T17:59:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:59:52+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://thinkworld-project.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have made substantial progress in robotic manipulation, but their generalization remains constrained by the diversity and coverage of action-labeled robot demonstrations. Human videos provide much broader behavioral and environmental experience, while learning from them faces a fundamental trade-off between scalable data and explicit action grounding. We introduce ThinkWorld, a VLA learning framework that grounds action reasoning in world dynamics and strengthens it through reinforcement learning with world imagination. ThinkWorld represents each action thought as a Chain-of-Dynamics (CoD), a sequence of discrete motion tokens that captures the intermediate dynamics connecting the current world to a future state and serves as a common action representation across embodiments. We first initialize CoD with action-labeled demonstrations, then predict the future induced by each CoD and reinforce those whose predictions better match the observed outcome. This enables videos without action annotations to provide direct supervision from observed state transitions, allowing human and robot experience to refine the same reasoning space. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate effective human-to-robot transfer and strong generalization to unseen tasks and environments.

Abstract
Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands is the next frontier of robotics, yet learned hand skills remain limited to simple grasps or basic repeated motions, such as rotating an object. As robots attempt more demanding in-hand maneuvers, safety, such as not dropping the object, becomes critical, yet it is rarely measured. We study in-hand object pose reaching, a task that demands diverse and difficult grasps: moving an object to almost any position and orientation within reach of the fingers, at any wrist orientation, through long sequences of consecutive goals. We present a recipe for training such policies for a LEAP hand in simulation without human data, built on a range of insights, from diverse resets and goals via generated grasp datasets to an adaptive friction curriculum. With careful system identification, the policies transfer zero-shot to the real hand, using either motion capture or a single wrist-mounted depth camera, and prove remarkably capable and robust. On top of the task policy, we add a learned residual safety filter with adjustable conservativeness, letting us tune the balance between performance and safety and greatly reduce object drops. In 13.3 hours of real-robot evaluation over 4,800 unseen goals across 4 objects and diverse wrist orientations, the state-based policy reaches 95.2% of goals within 2 cm and 0.4 rad, and the filter cuts its drops by 75% while still reaching 91.5% of goals. The policies can also spin a lightbulb held in an arbitrary pose, reach out-of-distribution goals, withstand fast arm motion and perturbations, and, needing only a wrist camera, serve as portable skills such as screwing in a lightbulb. We release the full recipe as a platform for studying performance, reliability, and safety in high-dimensional dexterous systems. Videos and more are available at https://ian-chuang.github.io/dexsafe/.

Abstract
Prediction of the transcriptional response of a cell to a genetic perturbation is a central problem in the computational modeling of gene regulation. Perturbations of the same gene can produce different transcriptional responses depending on the targeted locus, such as an alternative transcription start site or a distal regulatory element, and gene-level perturbation models represent these distinct interventions with a single gene identifier. We introduce STRAND, a generative model that encodes the regulatory DNA sequence at the perturbation locus and uses this encoding to parameterize a conditional transport process from control to perturbed single-cell states. Sequence conditioning enables zero-shot prediction for perturbation targets absent from training and supports locus-resolved inputs at 128 bp model resolution within a 524 kb sequence window. On CRISPRi screens in K562, Jurkat, and RPE1 cells, STRAND achieves the best average rank on few-shot and unseen-gene benchmarks, with gains of up to 33% in perturbation discrimination, and improves transfer to held-out cell lines by up to 0.12 in $Δ$Pearson. Ablation studies attribute these gains to sequence conditioning and to conditional transport. At the locus level, STRAND distinguishes alternative transcription start sites and validated regulatory elements that gene-identifier models represent as a single perturbation. STRAND is available at https://github.com/mims-harvard/STRAND.

Abstract
Agile robots require spatiotemporal intelligence to interpret scenes, reason about interactions, and anticipate future events. Yet it remains unclear how well foundation vision models support these capabilities, particularly from a moving egocentric camera, which is a natural view for robots. To investigate this, we collect an annotated tennis dataset and train a suite of vision models on spatiotemporal tasks. Our dataset contains egocentric video synchronized with five allocentric videos, estimated global positions, labeled contact times, and annotated stroke types. We then evaluate seven prediction tasks from egocentric images: 2D ego player and 3D ball localization, future player and ball trajectories, forehand or backhand stroke anticipation, time to the next ego player ball contact, and 3D ball position at that contact. We compare image encoders, video encoders, and vision-language models with jointly trained prediction heads. The best performing models achieve mean current-frame localization errors of 0.14 m for the ego player and 0.54 m for the ball, future forecasting errors of 0.34 m for the ego player and 0.91 m for the ball over 1.1 s, and a mean contact-time prediction error of 0.14 s. Auxiliary depth information substantially improves 3D ball localization, suggesting that inferring metric depth remains a challenge. Longer image histories do not improve ball forecasting, indicating that representations exploit scene context rather than rely solely on inter-frame motion. Finally, we propose a conditional diffusion model to generate whole-body kinematic tennis motion from vision encoder predictions on egocentric video at real-time rates, adapting movements as predictions are refined. These results move towards a deeper understanding of how foundation vision models can support real-time dynamic robots using egocentric video.

GAS-PFN: Graph-Aware Spatiotemporal Prior-Data Fitted Networks for Zero-Shot Traffic Forecasting

Abstract
Traffic forecasting requires modeling complex temporal dynamics and the spatial dependencies imposed by road topology. Supervised graph-based models achieve strong performance, but are often trained for a particular dataset and sensor graph, making deployment to new or evolving networks costly. Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs) offer a zero-shot alternative by learning in-context inference from synthetic task priors, which provide an explicit mechanism for encoding domain-specific assumptions. Existing forecasting PFNs, however, do not explicitly represent the graph-structured dependencies governing traffic dynamics. To combine graph-aware traffic modeling with zero-shot adaptation, we introduce the Graph-Aware Spatiotemporal Prior-Data Fitted Network (GAS-PFN). GAS-PFN incorporates sampled road topology into its synthetic task prior through random-walk diffusion, teaching the model to learn graph-coupled dynamics during pretraining. Its co-designed inference architecture uses two mask-conditioned passes through a shared Transformer backbone to separately model sensor-specific temporal dynamics and spatiotemporal interactions among neighboring sensors before integrating their representations for prediction. This design enables GAS-PFN to use target-network structure and context without parameter updates. Across six benchmarks at the 90-minute horizon, GAS-PFN reduces MSE by 34.0% on average relative to TimePFN, its closest PFN-based predecessor. It also reduces MSE by 27.7% on average relative to four recent industry-developed general-purpose zero-shot forecasters. In this comparison, all models receive 90 minutes of contiguous recent history, and any additional context is drawn from each dataset's chronological training split. These results indicate that aligning PFN priors and inference architectures with graph structure improves in-context learning for zero-shot traffic forecasting.

Gym-Anything-RT: Scaling Real-Time Environments for Vision-Language Agents

Abstract
AI agents have made substantial progress in domains where they have a non-trivial amount of time to deliberate between actions, such as mathematics, software engineering, and performing tasks on desktop applications. It is less clear how such models fare in settings that demand real-time interaction, such as playing a video game or operating a physical device. In these settings, the environment state may evolve as the agent is reasoning, or even as the underlying model is performing a forward pass. To gain insight into the real-time interaction capabilities of vision-language agents, we introduce Gym-Anything-RT. Our key idea is to construct visual minigames that demand a portfolio of capabilities required for interactive real-time settings. Gym-Anything-RT uses an agentic pipeline to automatically generate such environments based on a taxonomy of six capabilities required for real-time interaction, including the ability to perceive the environment and act under an evolving environment state. A key feature of Gym-Anything-RT is that it allows for controlled comparison of real-time interaction capabilities: we can run the same minigame in real time, or pause or slow down the environment to relax the requirement for real-time actions. We use Gym-Anything-RT to construct 100 real-time minigames and evaluate AI agents on them. We find large capability gaps in real-time interaction. For example, GPT-6 Astra (xHigh) achieves a pass rate of only 9.0% on the real-time minigames, which rises to 75.0% when the environment is paused. We also identify intriguing behaviors by the most powerful frontier agents: for instance, when given the ability to write code, these agents write code that bypasses the need to perceive and act in real time. We publicly release Gym-Anything-RT and the generated minigames to spur further research on AI agents in real-time interactive settings.

Abstract
Failure diagnosers for robot manipulation are increasingly vision--language models (VLMs), trained and evaluated on single-arm failures produced by perturbing a successful demonstration. Bimanual tool use adds failures of other kinds: a tool that slips out of its socket while still held, two correct gestures made at different times, and attempts that fail for two reasons at once. On a simulated bimanual tool-use task whose failure causes are read from the recorded trace, we measure what each sensing channel lets a diagnoser tell. The tool's slip is read on the tool hand; which hand was late is read on both arms, while a sensor on one hand reads its own hand's delay and at most part of the other's; and whether a late attempt was also pushed too weakly is not identifiable from passive sensing when the tool hand is late. Within two attempts, a repair policy that escalates when the cause is not identifiable repairs as many failures as one told the true cause. A VLM's internal state carries the late hand almost perfectly, along a direction no closer to the one its answer reads than a random one; whether the answer reports it depends on the wording of the question.

Abstract
Self-attention mechanisms have established the Transformer as the dominant backbone across natural language processing, source code intelligence, and automated theorem proving. However, all-to-all pairwise token comparison enforces a quadratic $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ compute and memory barrier alongside severe softmax attention dilution as sequence length $N$ scales beyond the training horizon. Inspired by recent advances in algebraic graph learning (\textbf{WPNet}), this work introduces \textbf{GTFormer}, a syntax-free hierarchical Graph Neural Network (GNN) that replaces flat self-attention for general long-context sequence modeling. GTFormer organizes raw token streams into a bounded-degree hierarchy combining a balanced binary reduction tree ($\lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ depth), horizontal boundary-bridging shift edges, random expander shortcuts, multi-channel convex highway gating, and a top-down broadcast pass. Across eight long-context code, log, and multi-hop retrieval benchmarks trained exclusively on sequences of length $N \le 512$, GTFormer extrapolates zero-shot to $N = 4{,}194{,}304$ ($8192\times$) on a single commodity GPU in linear $\mathcal{O}(N)$ memory, maintaining near-perfect accuracy where standard and RoPE Transformers suffer from out-of-distribution attention collapse and out-of-memory exhaustion.

Abstract
Many modern vision-language models (VLMs) build on autoregressive decoding of discrete tokens. While text-based output interfaces enable scalable pretraining and strong zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks, they are poorly suited for problems that require precise continuous outputs, such as localizing temporal boundaries of events or generating robotic control actions. To address this challenge, we propose DRIFT, a general framework for adapting pretrained VLMs to continuous decoding tasks. DRIFT combines a base predictor, which provides a coarse estimate of the target output, with a generative refinement module based on flow matching that iteratively improves the prediction. This residual formulation transforms the generative modeling problem from learning a global output distribution to modeling a localized residual distribution around a strong prior, substantially simplifying optimization. We evaluate DRIFT on both perception and planning tasks, including visual grounding and robotic control. Across multiple tasks and architectures spanning MLLMs, VLAs, and WAMs, DRIFT consistently outperforms a strong set of regression- and generative-based solutions.

Abstract
Photorealistic volume rendering of computed tomography (CT) may convey anatomy intuitively, but it needs the CT data, dedicated software and a powerful graphics processor. Three-dimensional Gaussian splatting (3DGS) displays photorealistic scenes in real time on less powerful devices, but existing medical approaches train each scene for minutes to hours. We aimed to convert a CT volume rendering directly into a 3DGS model without training. Splats were placed on an adaptive octree of millimetre-sized cells. Their colour and opacity were computed on the graphics processor with the renderer's lighting, cell opacity was converted analytically into splat opacity, and faint splats were retained stochastically above the 1/255 opacity floor of standard 3DGS rasterisers. Six public CT scans (1.5-mm voxels) were converted with three transfer functions and compared with converged references by peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity and a learned perceptual metric. Two-million-splat models reached a PSNR of 32.6 +/- 0.9 dB for bone, 30.1 +/- 1.0 dB for contrast-enhanced vessels and 32.3 +/- 1.5 dB for skin and bone. A conversion pass (usually two or three per model) took 4-30 s on a current laptop and 9-60 s on a desktop with a 4-GB graphics card. On the laptop, the models rendered at 68 frames per second at 800 x 800 pixels. Training-free conversion produced real-time 3DGS models of photorealistic CT renderings in about a minute or less per conversion pass on ordinary computers, with fidelity that we consider sufficient for interactive viewing.

Hierarchical Sparse Graph for Multi-dimensional Assignment in Event-based Tracking

Abstract
Existing event-based tracking methods typically operate in an online manner, associating tracklets across adjacent batches. However, this limited temporal window often leads to failures in challenging scenarios, such as long-term occlusions or missed detections, where broader temporal context should be incorporated for high-precision tracking. To achieve robust and accurate tracking, this paper concentrates on the event-based multi-dimensional assignment that associates tracklets across multiple batches simultaneously. Unlike image-based detectors, event-based detectors can generate multiple correct yet spatially distinct tracklets for a single object per batch. This violates the one-to-one rule in classic data association, making event-based association an underdetermined many-to-many matching problem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to tackle this NP-hard problem. To address its underdetermined nature, we formulate the assignment problem via fuzzy logic, where pairwise tracklet correlations are quantified through multiple long-range kinematic metrics. This fuzzy design eliminates the need for hard tracking thresholds and enables multiple heterogeneous metrics to be integrated into a unified correlation. By treating tracklets as nodes and their fuzzy correlations as edges, we construct a Hierarchical Sparse Graph (HSG). Building upon the HSG, we pose assignment as a graph partition task, and employ a heuristic solution that measures vertex affinity for partitioning adaptively. The entire pipeline is training-free. Comprehensive experiments on two challenging event datasets, eTram and Ev-UAV, show that our method outperforms existing event-based and most image-based association models. Particularly, it achieves over 2$\times$ the performance of OC-SORT across all metrics in the many-to-many association scenario.

Abstract

Existing processing methods primarily follow two paradigms, pseudo-frames and event-by-event. Abstract
Recent advances in diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in restoring high-quality images in a zero-shot manner. Unfortunately, extending this success to video restoration is challenging due to the substantial computational resources required to pre-train video diffusion models. A rising trend seeks to address video restoration by utilizing a pre-trained image diffusion model, initiating restoration from either frame-wise independent or identical Gaussian noise. Yet, these approaches often fall short, as such simple noise initiations fail to capture and utilize the inherent temporal correlations within degraded video frames. In this work, we propose taming noise initialization for zero-shot video restoration, which preserves the intrinsic temporal correlations by generating initial noises directly using DDIM inversion on the corrupted video frames themselves. Extensive experiments on multiple video restoration tasks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing zero-shot approaches, consistently achieving superior results on the DAVIS, REDS4, and Set8 datasets.

Abstract
End-to-end video quality assessment (VQA) methods leave the regional evidence underlying their predictions largely implicit. We introduce an explicit pixel-level regional representation for VQA, instantiated as a Quality-Relevance Heatmap (QRH) that combines localized degradation evidence with perceptual priors under vision-language model guidance, without requiring pixel-level annotations. We apply QRH to existing VQA models through two deterministic fragment-sampling strategies: distribution-preserving sampling approximates the original sampler's QRH-score distribution without retraining, while quality-aware sampling prioritizes regions with high QRH scores and is paired with fine-tuning. Experiments with DOVER on multiple benchmarks show that the former closely approximates predictions averaged over random samples, while the latter improves most evaluated metrics across popular VQA benchmarks. These results support explicit regional representations as a useful interface for downstream VQA.

Abstract
Transformers achieve state-of-the-art performance in language processing and computer vision. Their computation increasingly depends on hardware that can keep pace not only with large matrix multiplications, but also with nonlinear operations like Softmax within the core attention mechanism. On digital accelerators, Softmax accounts for only a small fraction of total operations, yet it can disproportionately impact overall inference latency. Here, we use thin-film lithium niobate Mach-Zehnder modulators (MZM) as analog computing elements for low-latency Transformer attention. Our high-speed nonlinearities based on the intrinsic sinusoidal MZM response achieve performance close to digital baselines in Vision Transformers and GPT-2, even under 4-bit input and output quantization. We experimentally characterize the electro-optic response at symbol rates up to 10 GBaud and assess model robustness under corresponding noise conditions. Accounting for the full signal chain, our analysis indicates more than an order of magnitude lower latency relative to previous custom electronic and photonic Softmax implementations. These results establish analog electro-optic devices as nonlinear function units well suited for integration into emerging co-packaged electronic-photonic hardware, offering a path toward high-speed Transformer inference.

Abstract
Robust anatomical segmentation of chest X-rays (CXRs) remains challenging due to the scarcity of comprehensive annotations and the substantial variability of real-world acquisition conditions. We propose AnyCXR, a unified framework that enables generalizable multi-organ segmentation across arbitrary CXR projection angles using only synthetic supervision. The method combines a Multi-stage Domain Randomization (MSDR) engine, which generates over 100,000 anatomically faithful and highly diverse synthetic radiographs from 3D CT volumes, with a Conditional Joint Annotation Regularization (CAR) learning strategy that leverages partial and imperfect labels by enforcing anatomical consistency in a latent space. Trained entirely on synthetic data, AnyCXR achieves strong zero-shot generalization on multiple real-world datasets, providing accurate delineation of 54 anatomical structures in PA, lateral, and oblique views. The resulting segmentation maps support downstream clinical tasks, including automated cardiothoracic ratio estimation, spine curvature assessment, and disease classification, where the incorporation of anatomical priors improves diagnostic performance. These results demonstrate that AnyCXR establishes a scalable and reliable foundation for anatomy-aware CXR analysis and offers a practical pathway toward reducing annotation burdens while improving robustness across diverse imaging conditions.

Abstract
Learning from experience is essential for building the next generation of agents that continually expand their capabilities. Pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) already internalize rich world knowledge, yet this knowledge is rarely leveraged to drive policy improvement through online interaction. Realizing this potential efficiently remains challenging, as policy updates shift the agent's interaction distribution, requiring the world model to adapt accordingly, while continually updating large pretrained models is computationally expensive. To address this challenge, we introduce Dwell, a model-based reinforcement learning (RL) framework that reframes a VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, endowing it with the ability to predict, adjust, and act in latent space. Across five interactive visual environments, Dwell achieves the strongest overall performance among trainable methods while substantially reducing the compute required for imagination, showing that effective world models for VLM agents need not reconstruct the future; they need to predict futures that are useful for action. Abstract
GUI grounding on high-resolution screens fails mostly on small targets, and zooming in is a common training-free remedy. However, most existing zooming pipelines do not match the zoom to the target's size, accept the zoomed answer unconditionally and never revisit a wrong answer. Abstract
Monocular 6-DoF spacecraft pose estimation methods predominantly process individual frames, discarding the temporal information present in an image sequence acquired during spacecraft manoeuvres. Few temporal approaches require full backbone fine-tuning or auxiliary optical flow networks, risking catastrophic forgetting or increasing computational cost, respectively. Abstract
Simulation-to-reality translation must bridge the appearance gap between synthetic and real domains while preserving structural and semantic consistency. Conditioning-based methods achieve spatial alignment but introduce computationally expensive control modules. Paired-data methods achieve realism but rely on complex synthesis pipelines, often altering scene geometry and semantics. Training-free editing methods avoid both constraints but lack a learned appearance prior, limiting their perceptual quality. Recently proposed phase-preserving diffusion presents a promising alternative, but Fourier-domain formulations are constrained by global spectral coupling. This coupling induces spatial artifacts such as ringing and boundary leakage, thereby degrading structural and semantic consistency. We introduce Wavelet Phase Diffusion, which addresses this through two components. First, we operate in the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Packet Transform domain, whose localized wavelet packets enable spatially adaptive phase injection without global spectral interference. Second, Low-Frequency Randomization (LFR) replaces the low-frequency packet, decoupling the model from the synthetic illumination prior and enabling in-distribution real-world appearance. Both components train on unpaired open-domain data, and introduce negligible inference overhead. The spatial locality further enables instance-level translation, where individual objects or regions are translated to photorealistic appearance independently while the surrounding scene remains untranslated. On vKITTI $\to$ KITTI image translation, $ψ$-PD outperforms prior methods in realism and semantic consistency while maintaining competitive structural alignment. For CARLA video translation, $ψ$-PD approaches the realism of paired-data methods while reducing VLM planner ADE and FDE by $5.4\%$ and $5.1\%$, respectively.

Abstract
Diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) process their entire input at every denoising step. Since visual tokens make up 89-97% of this input on the benchmarks we study, most of the decoding computation is spent repeatedly processing the same image. Visual token pruning methods partially relieve this burden by removing part of the visual tokens, but they lose the removed information and still process the remaining image at every step. To measure how often the image is actually needed, we compare LLaDA-V's predictions with and without the image at every decoding step and find that removing the image changes a token being unmasked at only 22.8% of steps on average and at as few as 7.9%. We therefore propose LOWN, a decoding method that decides at every step whether to process the image and how many tokens to unmask. LOWN predicts the masked tokens without the image and processes the image only when this prediction disagrees with the last image-conditioned prediction. Agreement certifies the earlier prediction for reuse, and the number of certified tokens sets how many are unmasked in that step. We further observe that the need for the image differs across benchmarks, so LOWN's adaptive decision is needed rather than a fixed schedule. Without training or a tuned threshold, LOWN retains 99.6% of LLaDA-V's score on ten benchmarks at 35.3% of the decoding FLOPs on average. LOWN also generalizes across LLaDA-based DVLMs, composes orthogonally with visual token pruning, and runs under key-value caching for further efficiency gains.
