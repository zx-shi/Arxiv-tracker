On Agentic Manipulation Policies
Authors: Chen Chen, Omid Bagherieh, Naveen Nikamanth Appiah Balaji, Jesse T. Gonzalez, Negin Heravi, Fahad Islam, Xueting Li, Jean-Baptiste Regli, Kejia Ren, Petr Švec, David J. Yoon, Matthias Müller, Vladlen Koltun

Abstract
Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6\% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2\% and 42.9\% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The agent succeeds zero-shot in 104 of 108 real-world trials spanning twelve tasks and three robot instances, reducing failure rates by 20x and 15x relative to strong VLA and WAM baselines. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation.

Abstract
Zeolites are an important class of porous crystalline materials with broad applications in gas storage, separations, and catalysis. Although hundreds of thousands of experimentally realized and hypothetical zeolite structures are known, identifying materials optimized for a target application remains challenging because property labels are expensive to obtain and available only for limited structure-molecule pairs. While prior work has demonstrated that 3D neural representations can predict zeolite properties effectively, it remains unclear which representation-learning strategies are most effective in the low-data regime relevant to materials discovery. We present ZeoBench, a benchmark for self-supervised learning on dense 3D representations of all-silica zeolites, and evaluate the resulting features on six existing downstream adsorption-property prediction tasks, across a range of training set sizes. Our study compares self-supervised 3D pretraining on volumetric zeolite data, adaptations of image-pretrained 2D ConvNets and vision transformers to 3D via multiview and channel-inflation strategies, and 3D ConvNets pretrained on out-of-domain volumetric datasets, alongside hand-crafted descriptors and supervised baselines. Using our pretraining and evaluation framework, we find self-supervised 3D representations outperform existing methods and achieve state-of-the-art performance across both in-domain label regimes and for the unseen domain of metal organic frameworks. Learned representations consistently outperform hand-crafted descriptors, and convolutional architectures are generally more effective than vision transformers in this setting. These results establish ZeoBench as a practical benchmark for 3D representation learning in zeolites and provide guidance for data-limited adsorption property prediction.

AR-Edit: Training-Free Autoregressive Video Editing without Inversion
Authors: Hovhannes Margaryan, Quentin Bammey, Christian Sandor, Vicky Kalogeiton
First: 2026-11-06T17:50:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T17:50:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Editing a video stream in real time, without access to future frames, is essential for interactive applications. Existing training-free video editing methods, however, assume full offline access to the entire sequence, while streaming approaches either rely on additional training or are limited to specific editing techniques. We show that this limitation can be removed by analyzing the self-attention dynamics of DMD-distilled autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models. We uncover a key asymmetry: queries and keys remain stable and aligned with their source counterparts across denoising timesteps, while values diverge and encode the evolving content. Inherited from the non-distilled bidirectional teacher, this asymmetry is strongest at high noise, where few-step streaming models operate. The resulting mechanism is simple: a single forward pass suffices to capture source identity in the value features, which can be reused throughout generation. Starting from pure Gaussian noise and injecting only these source values reconstructs the input with fidelity matching or surpassing inversion-based methods, effectively eliminating inversion. Based on this insight, we introduce AR-Edit, a training-free, inversion-free method that caches source values once and selectively injects them during denoising. Across multiple AR backbones, AR-Edit enables real-time streaming editing with superior source preservation and tonal fidelity at minimal overhead.

Abstract
When should a robot look at the cup, and when at the portafilter? Spatial-reasoning benchmarks evaluate whether a model can describe a scene, but manipulation needs something more specific: the ability to acquire the particular evidence that makes the current command executable. A view that is informative for one task can be useless for another, even at the same workstation. We introduce Seeing What Matters, a benchmark for task-conditioned actionable spatial belief in simulated tabletop manipulation. Across three domains--coffee workstation, drawer/cabinet, and microwave/appliance--the benchmark provides 860 frozen evaluation cases; expanding across methods, budgets, and inference repeats yields more than 115,000 evaluator episodes under two protocols: closed-loop actionability and same-object functional out-of-distribution generalization. Each case pairs a set of candidate RGB views with a manipulation command, shared scripted skills, and hidden postconditions, so the benchmark directly measures whether a view-selection policy acquires the observations its command actually needs. We also provide a diagnostic reference pipeline that maintains a spatial-functional belief state and ranks views by missing evidence. At primary budget settings, this pipeline reaches 98.8% closed-loop and 98.9% functional-OOD success; ablating the functional or command-binding components collapses performance to near zero. Seven vision-language models, evaluated on the same cases, reach only 26-43%. Replacing the reference pipeline's coverage metadata with frozen RGB-derived scores preserves 98.5%/98.9% success; shuffling the visual scores collapses it to 15%/11%, confirming that the result depends on image content. These gaps suggest that spatial belief for manipulation should be evaluated not by scene-level completeness, but by whether it acquires the evidence the current command requires.

Abstract
The recent surge of interest in Time Series Foundation Models has rapidly advanced the field. Flow-Direct: Feedback-Efficient and Reusable Guidance for Flow Models via Non-Parametric Guidance Field
Authors: Kim Yong Tan, Yueming Lyu, Ivor Tsang, Yew-Soon Ong
First: 2026-05-08T04:03:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T16:36:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Training-free guidance enables pre-trained diffusion and flow models to optimize application-specific objectives using feedback from external black-box reward functions. However, existing methods are feedback-inefficient because reward feedback is used only transiently to inform a localized gradient approximation or a discrete search decision, and is subsequently discarded. To address this limitation, we propose Flow-Direct, a framework that guides the generation process via a persistent guidance field. Theoretically, this guidance field is analytically derived from the log-density ratio between the base and reward-weighted target distributions; it transports the pre-trained distribution to the target distribution. In practice, the field is implemented as a non-parametric estimator constructed from all accumulated reward-evaluated samples. As more samples are collected during optimization, this empirical guidance field becomes increasingly accurate. This persistent formulation yields two major advantages. First, Flow-Direct is highly feedback-efficient: because every evaluated sample is used to refine the global guidance field, no reward information is wasted. Second, the framework is naturally reusable: once optimization is complete, the collected dataset defines a reusable guidance field for generating novel target samples without additional reward evaluations, and distinct guidance fields can be combined to generate samples that simultaneously satisfy multiple objectives.

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have become a promising paradigm for end-to-end robot control, but their effectiveness still depends heavily on large volumes of demonstration data. In manipulation tasks, this dependence is compounded by structured spatiotemporal uncertainty in the action space: multiple nearby actions or trajectories may successfully solve the same task, while semantically equivalent demonstrations often unfold at different speeds. Standard pointwise supervision treats each demonstrated action as an exact target, imposing an unnecessary learning burden under such uncertainty. We present SURE, a data-efficient VLA training framework that explicitly models both spatial and temporal uncertainty. SURE augments a VLA backbone with a lightweight transposed-convolutional probabilistic action decoder that captures spatial uncertainty through local correlations among neighboring action bins. It further introduces Soft-DTW alignment supervision to accommodate local timing variations between predicted and demonstrated action chunks. Together, these two components reduce sensitivity to demonstrator-specific poses and execution timing, enabling more data-efficient action learning. We evaluate SURE on LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world manipulation tasks. Even with only 10% of the demonstration data or 20% of the training steps, SURE achieves manipulation success rates that match or surpass baseline VLAs.

Abstract
With the proliferation of AI-generated images, digital watermarking has become an essential safeguard for protecting intellectual property and mitigating malicious exploitation. Recent works on semantic watermarking have enabled efficient copyright protection for diffusion models. However, the dependence of semantic watermarking on diffusion inversion for watermark detection creates a critical vulnerability. Imprint removal and forgery attacks exploit this weakness to produce deceptive results. Our analysis reveals that these attacks succeed by displacing watermarked latents into the unwatermarked region, while guiding unwatermarked latents into the watermarked region. Based on that, we propose Progressive Guided Inversion and Denoising (PGID), the first plug-and-play, training-free noise extraction framework designed to defend against both attack strategies. PGID effectively defends by projecting perturbed latents back to the region where they originally belong. The projection is achieved by eliminating intermediate latent deflections and mitigating adversarial perturbations through progressive inversion-denoising cycles. Comprehensive evaluations across multiple schemes demonstrate that PGID successfully restores detection reliability by recovering removed watermarks and identifying forged instances.

Abstract
Particle image velocimetry (PIV) reconstructs a flow field from the motion of tracer particles but the reconstruction quality is bounded by how well the flow structure of interest is resolved in the images. The acquisition position is typically fixed by the operator before the measurement. We cast camera placement as a sequential decision-making problem and train a reinforcement learning agent to control a 6-DoF manipulator equipped with a camera, enabling it to locate, frame and resolve a hydrofoil wake in a water tunnel. The agent commands the camera pose and observes the scene only through the velocity field and uncertainty estimated from time-separated image pairs. It is trained entirely in a photorealistic simulation of our water tunnel, with wakes generated by Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations of hydrofoils with varying profiles, positions and angles of attack. After zero-shot transfer to the real water tunnel, the policy reliably locates and frames the wakes of randomly placed hydrofoils. It resolves the wake at a higher resolution than a fixed camera view and with lower error in the measured flow statistics, in roughly half the time of a full scan and with far fewer image pairs.

Abstract
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has been increasingly adopted to fine-tune pretrained models with low computational cost and is therefore also preferred in the federated learning paradigm to reduce computational and communication overhead. In this work, we investigate the sparsely explored problem of the risk of private data leakage in LoRA-adapted Vision Transformers (ViTs), where low-rank updates weaken the gradient signal available to a server with access to individual client updates. We show that quality of reconstruction is limited by two coupled ambiguities: reduced information in LoRA gradients and missing spatial cues from frozen positional embeddings. To address this, we propose a spatially anchored generative inversion framework that incorporates server-prescribed trainable positional embeddings into ViT blocks during fine-tuning at clients. The resulting positional gradients act as layer-wise spatial anchors, providing additional positional cues, while the generative prior keeps reconstructions on a realistic image manifold, reducing duplicate-data and patch-permutation artifacts, {which produce similar gradients as those produced when the target client images are used}. Adding our proposed spatial anchoring to GIAS improves PSNR by 10.14 dB, SSIM by 0.45, and LPIPS by 0.098 on CIFAR-10, as well as PSNR gains of 7.83 and 11.74 dB on high-resolution CelebA and CARS datasets, respectively, in the FedSGD setting. Such gains in reconstructed image quality metrics are also maintained at higher batch sizes and in FedAvg settings. These results indicate that LoRA-based PEFT does not, by itself, prevent data leakage in FL, and carefully incorporated positional embeddings can help in exposing private information.

Abstract

Abstract
Diffusion models generate samples by denoising along the score of a perturbed target distribution. In practice, one trains a neural diffusion model, which is computationally expensive. Recent work suggests that score matching implicitly smooths the empirical score, and that this smoothing bias promotes generalization by capturing low-dimensional data geometry. We propose moment-matched score-smoothed overdamped Langevin dynamics (MM-SOLD), a training-free interacting particle sampler that enforces the target moments throughout the sampling trajectory. We prove that the one-particle marginal of the ideal constrained Gibbs equilibrium converges, as the number of particles grows, to a Gibbs--Boltzmann density obtained by exponentially tilting the naive score-smoothed target. Its mean and covariance match the training GMM, even for rank-deficient data. We additionally establish sampling consistency, statistical target-error bounds for bounded data, and consistency of a safeguarded BAOAB-based tilting estimator. Experiments on 2D distributions and latent-space image generation show that MM-SOLD enables fast, robust, latent sampling on CPUs using fixed pretrained autoencoders, with sample fidelity and diversity competitive with neural diffusion baselines.

Abstract
Vision-language-action models are typically built on a simple premise: perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. Abstract

Abstract
Semantic change detection in remote sensing aims to identify land cover changes between bi-temporal image pairs. Progress in this area has been limited by the scarcity of annotated datasets, as pixel-level annotation is costly and time-consuming. To address this, recent methods leverage synthetic data or generate artificial change pairs, but transferring change detection models across datasets and acquisition conditions remains challenging. In this work, we introduce a weak temporal supervision strategy that leverages additional temporal observations of existing single-temporal datasets, without requiring any new annotations. Specifically, we extend single-date remote sensing datasets with new observations acquired at different times and train a change detection model by assuming that real bi-temporal pairs mostly contain no change, while pairing images from different locations to generate change examples.
