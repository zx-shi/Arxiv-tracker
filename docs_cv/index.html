SUREON: A Benchmark and Vision-Language Model for Surgical Reasoning
Authors: Alejandra Perez, Anita Rau, Busisiwe Mlambo, Lee White, Aina Brunet, Juan Verde, Juan Caicedo, Joël L. Lavanchy, Adam Schmidt, Chinedu Nwoye, Omid Mohareri, Muhammad Abdullah Jamal

Abstract
A capable surgical AI model should reason from visual evidence; yet, many existing surgical benchmarks can be partially solved without visual input, as models may exploit procedural priors or common anatomy-instrument interactions to infer answers from language alone. To address this, we introduce SUREON, a video question-answering (QA) dataset and benchmark designed to evaluate visually grounded surgical reasoning. Our pipeline generates QA pairs from narrated surgical academic videos, refines incorrect options using multiple models to reduce linguistic biases, and uses trained adversarial guessers to filter out questions answerable without visual evidence. The resulting dataset contains 76K training pairs covering 168 procedures and 10 specialties, alongside a benchmark of 340 questions across 10 categories verified by expert surgeons. Benchmarking leading commercial and open-source models shows that surgical visual reasoning remains largely unsolved--no model exceeds 53% accuracy and even expert surgeons reach only 64.9%. We also introduce SUREON-VLM, a baseline trained on our curated data that avoids language shortcuts, outperforms existing open-source models on SUREON, and matches larger specialist models on multiple standard surgical benchmarks. Our training ablations show that distractor refinement and blind curation strengthen visual grounding, increasing the multimodal gain from 8.7 to 29.1 percentage points while reducing the training set from 176.6K to 76K filtered samples. SUREON thus offers both a verified testbed for surgical visual reasoning and training data that strengthens visual grounding.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by having a draft propose tokens that a target verifies in parallel. Frontier models increasingly ship a built-in Multi-Token-Prediction (MTP) draft head, assumed to be cheap beside verification. A shipped full-attention MTP head can become disproportionately expensive beside a hybrid target at long context. The head attends over the full KV cache for each of $γ$ draft forwards per decode step, while only 9-25% of target layers retain full attention, so a single draft layer can issue context reads at a rate comparable to the whole target. On hard, low-acceptance workloads, deep native MTP becomes net-negative: slower than no speculation. We apply a StreamingLLM-style window plus attention sink to the draft only (Windowed-MTP); target verification remains full-context, so the target distribution is preserved. The intervention is training-free and drop-in. On three models from two hybrid families (Gated DeltaNet and Mamba2) at 1M context on one GPU in SGLang, it provides an acceptance-free 1.28-1.44x per-decode-step speedup over native MTP. The speedup grows with context; at matched acceptance, TPOT improves by the same factor. The window removes most of the draft's working set, and a compact ring buffer converts that reduction into physical capacity: it reclaims the otherwise dead draft pool, a measured 7.7-11.1% of total KV at 1M, making room for one additional concurrent 1M request in the Qwen-35B batch sweep.

Abstract
A universal controller for any robot morphology would greatly improve computational and data efficiency. Steps have been made towards such multi-robot control by utilizing contextual information about the properties of individual robots and exploiting their modular structure in the architecture of deep reinforcement learning agents. When the robots have highly dissimilar morphologies, however, this becomes a challenging problem, especially when the agent must generalize to new, unseen robots. In this paper, we consider the fact that contextual features are often only partially available, but that they can be recovered through modular interactions. This can allow for better multi-robot control and generalization to contexts that are not seen during training. To this extent, we implement a transformer-based architecture with shared modular recurrence and evaluate its (generalization) performance on a large set of MuJoCo robots. The results show a substantial improvement in zero-shot generalization performance on robots with unseen dynamics, kinematics, and topologies, in four different environments.

Abstract
Vision language models (VLMs) are expected to perform effective multimodal reasoning and make logically coherent decisions, which is critical to tasks such as diagram understanding and spatial problem solving. However, current VLM reasoning lacks large-scale and well-structured training datasets. To bridge this gap, we propose VisualSphinx, a first-of-its-kind large-scale synthetic visual logical reasoning training dataset. Abstract
Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.

Abstract
Accurate forecasting with uncertainty quantification is critical in a variety of scientific applications ranging from epidemiology and economics to energy systems and earth sciences. Yet most forecasting models still need to be developed and trained separately for each domain-specific dataset. Time series foundation models (TSFMs) aim to remove this limitation and enable the application of the same model across domains through large-scale pretraining. However, most existing TSFMs treat each time series independently and therefore cannot model interactions among time series and external drivers. Here, we introduce Chronos-2, a foundation model for zero-shot forecasting that models the interactions between multivariate time series and heterogeneous covariates, which commonly arise in scientific applications. The model infers relationships among time series directly from historical context and generates probabilistic forecasts without task-specific treatment. Across various benchmarks, Chronos-2 achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on average by improving skill scores by 5.2 percentage points relative to the strongest baselines and increasing win rates by 10 percentage points. Chronos-2 is also computationally efficient, generating forecasts for 100 time series in under one second on a single mid-range GPU, 50x and 1000x faster end-to-end than the best task-specific point and probabilistic forecasters, respectively. In addition, we demonstrate its effectiveness in diverse scientific settings -- including infectious-disease forecasting, macroeconomic modeling, carbon-flux prediction, and runoff simulation -- where the model matches or surpasses specialized methods developed for each domain. These results show that a single pretrained model can capture structure shared across temporal systems.

Authors: Yin Yi, Haiping Ma, Siyu Song, Ziwen Wang, Shangshang Yang

Abstract
Knowledge tracing (KT) predicts a student's next response from their history. New platforms often have question texts but no response logs for training, motivating zero-shot cross-domain KT. Existing transfer and cold-start methods use question semantics, but cross-platform semantic discrepancies limit transferability. We propose TIZSKT, which separates behavioral sequence modeling from domain-local historical evidence construction and uses text as an index rather than a directly transferable predictive representation. The Mastery-Dynamics Encoder learns correctness dynamics without question identifiers or text embeddings. Domain-Local Question Addressing builds fixed question addresses using a frozen text encoder and an unsupervised per-domain projection. The Text-Addressing Kernel uses these addresses to aggregate the current student's previous outcomes into a question-specific historical correctness summary. Cross-Segment Continuation retains access to earlier responses without extending the behavioral encoder's input sequence. Evidence-Volume Read-Out preserves the total kernel mass discarded by normalization and combines its log-compressed value with the behavioral state and historical correctness summary to predict the next response. All trainable parameters are learned from source domains; target inference uses public question texts and the current student's observed history without target-domain training. Leave-one-domain-out experiments on three public datasets show that TIZSKT achieves the highest mean AUC and accuracy among evaluated baselines on all target domains. Ablations confirm the contributions of text addressing and cross-segment continuation.

Abstract
Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has advanced the automated design of deep networks, but remains computationally expensive due to reliance on model training or performance predictors. Abstract
Vision Transformers represent each image patch through a canonical token produced under one observation condition. That token is a powerful interface, but it is still one realized state of the patch. We introduce PMTT, which establishes two foundations: a patch representation object can extend from one canonical state to a canonical-centered Representation State Family, and identity-preserving variation opens Patch-Level Local Feature Space. We develop four parts of this representation program. Patch Information Acquisition and Dense Orbital Views expose lawful same-patch states. Visible-to-Hidden prediction tests whether observed states reveal structure beyond themselves. Intrinsic analysis identifies low-dimensional, energy-concentrated, patch-specific, predictive geometry. Representation Organization separates acquiring more information from organizing that information, with an analytic Manifold as the central structured representation. Across two independent 4,096-anchor cohorts, Manifold explains 70.8-79.6% of Hidden variation, compared with 55.0-72.3% for directly Observed states, and wins on more than 95% of anchors at every scale. Effective rank falls from about 2.5 to 2.0 while dominant energy concentration rises with backbone scale. Across six visual capabilities and four scales, Observed exceeds the canonical Center in 21 of 24 conditions, Manifold exceeds Center in 23, and Manifold exceeds Observed in 16. The strongest current consequence appears in physical correspondence, where MegaDepth satisfies Center < Observed < Manifold at every scale. These results show that pretrained visual backbones contain richer patch information than the standard single-token interface directly exposes. Abstract
Informative uncertainty quantification (UQ) is essential for the practical deployment of machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs), as it provides insight into their reliability across chemical space. Although numerous post-hoc uncertainty estimation methods for MLIPs have recently been proposed, most have been trained in low-data regimes, restricting their applicability to narrow domains of interest. Inspired by the emergence of foundational MLIPs, we propose pre-training uncertainty estimators on large, diverse datasets to enable broader applicability. Abstract
Multi-head attention in Vision Transformers (ViTs) allows independent attention patterns across heads, but without explicit regularization, heads may learn redundant patterns rather than complementary roles. Correlation-based penalties encourage head disentanglement, yet apply uniform regularization across all training samples. In face recognition (FR), hard samples whose embeddings lie close to competing identities are critical for optimization and generalization. Motivated by this, we propose ViT-HarDis, which conditions attention head disentanglement on sample difficulty: samples with high cosine similarity to negative class centers receive stronger head decorrelation, enforcing specialization where identity ambiguity is highest. Experiments show improvements over both the baseline and uniform disentanglement on most benchmarks. Trained on WebFace4M, ViT-HarDis achieves SOTA results among ViT-based methods on IJB-B, IJB-C, and TinyFace Rank-1, while achieving the best IJB-B and TinyFace results when trained on MS1MV3, improving FR without architectural modifications or additional inference cost. Abstract
Bridging independent vision-language models (VLMs) and small language models (SLMs) in a modular multi-agent system typically forces them to exchange information as discrete text, which creates a lossy bottleneck for spatial and fine-grained details. This constraint is especially acute for SLMs, whose limited capacity makes them more reliant on the quality of the input signal. Abstract
Adversarial patches can disrupt Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by manipulating visual observations, leading to failures in robot control. However, it remains poorly understood which internal mechanisms underlie these failures and how targeted interventions can mitigate them. In this work, we mechanistically analyze VLA representations using a sparse autoencoder (SAE) and identify a feature whose activation strongly correlates with the presence of an adversarial patch. Based on this analysis, we suppress the identified feature at inference time only when a linear probe detects an attack. This intervention improves robustness without the cost of fine-tuning the VLA. These results show that attack-related internal representations can provide useful targets for VLA adversarial defense and that controlling when to intervene is important for limiting disruption to nominal policy behavior.

Abstract
Long-horizon consistency is essential for video world models, yet it becomes difficult to maintain once observed content falls outside the context window. Abstract
Object detection models rely on large-scale annotated datasets that require substantial manual annotation effort. Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) have made significant progress in spatial reasoning, particularly by integrating RGB images with depth data, which is often assumed to faithfully reflect physical distances. However, mirrors violate this assumption: they distort depth readings while reproducing object appearances, making reflections difficult to distinguish from real objects. As existing benchmarks rarely consider such reflective scenarios, VLM performance in mirror scenes remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce a mirror-centric RGB-D dataset from reflective scenes to evaluate the spatial reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs in these scenarios. Specifically, we collect 1,011 RGB-D images from 525 real-world scenes, spanning diverse indoor and outdoor environments, six reflective surface types, and both daytime and nighttime conditions. All images are annotated with reflective surface region masks and mask annotations for both directly observed and mirror-reflected objects. Then, we curate a benchmark named MIRA-Bench (Mirror-Induced Reasoning Ambiguity Benchmark), comprising 14,140 QA pairs across eight sub-tasks organized into three cognitive levels: Reflection-aware Perception, Spatial Reasoning, and Scene-level Decision-making. We evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on MIRA-Bench and find consistent failures across tasks: models misclassify whether objects are directly observed or reflected, fail to match reflected instances to their real-world counterparts, and produce erroneous spatial inferences. Remarkably, providing depth data actually degrades reasoning performance, implying that VLMs cannot identify incorrect depth in mirror scenes.
