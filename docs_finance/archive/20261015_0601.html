Superdiffusive Invariance Principle for the two-dimensional Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process
Authors: Quentin Moulard
First: 2026-10-13T17:57:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:57:59+00:00
Comments: 85 pages, 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We consider the Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (ASEP) at its critical dimension $d=2$, started from the Bernoulli product measure with density $ρ\in (0,1)$. We first derive the sharp $\log(t)^{2/3}$ divergence of the bulk diffusivity, predicted via mode-coupling theory in [H. van Beijeren, R. Kutner, H. Spohn, Phys. Rev. Lett., 1985], including the exact prefactor, thereby removing the subleading diverging multiplicative corrections in the Tauberian bounds of [H.-T. Yau, Ann. of Math., 2004]. More importantly, we establish an invariance principle for the density fluctuation field on the corresponding superdiffusive scales. On the time scale $τ$, we rescale space by $\sqrtτ \log(τ)^{1/3}$ in the direction of the drift and by $\sqrtτ$ in the transverse direction, and prove functional convergence of the rescaled fluctuation process to the solution of a conservative stochastic heat equation with additive noise. This is the same explicit Gaussian Fixed Point obtained in [G. Cannizzaro, Q. Moulard, F. Toninelli, arXiv:2501.00344, 2025] for the two-dimensional Stochastic Burgers Equation (SBE). We also deduce a superdiffusive central limit theorem for the second-class particle under the same anisotropic rescaling. The result holds for any finite-range jump kernel with nonzero drift and uniaxial asymmetry. This is the first space-time scaling limit result for a nonequilibrium interacting particle system at the critical dimension, where Gaussian fluctuations emerge despite logarithmic corrections to the scaling. The proof uses an approximate fluctuation-dissipation relation, which is obtained by combining a sharp quantitative comparison between the ASEP and SBE generators with refined estimates on the SBE Ansatz. An additional key novelty is a tightness argument that overcomes the limitations of the Itô trick on superdiffusive scales.

The Last Remote Sensing Model Built by Humans? Casting Any Earth Observation Task as Recursive Self-Improvement
Authors: Sijie Zhao, Zeqi Zhou, Heli Qi, Xuanchao Fu, Zhuo Zheng, Junshi Xia, Junjue Wang, Weihao Xuan, Naoto Yokoya
First: 2026-10-13T17:57:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:57:51+00:00
Comments: Ongoing project
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Any verifiable task can be cast as a harness and a verifier, where the harness defines what the agent may use and the verifier defines what counts as success. Every EO task can be made verifiable, through labeled benchmarks, independent sensors, physical models or the repeated observation of the same place. Given only these two components, a general-purpose coding agent builds the EO model and improves it recursively on its own outputs. Settings long treated as separate research problems, from learning with a handful of labels to adapting across domains, become choices of harness and verifier. On four EO benchmarks spanning optical, SAR and multispectral time-series imagery, agent-built models surpass the state of the art and keep most of that accuracy from about 1% of the training labels. This establishes a new paradigm for EO, in which people design harnesses and verifiers and agents build the models, so EO models improve as agents do.

Abstract
Simulation-based Bayesian inference for Chemical Reaction Networks (CRNs) must simultaneously solve two computationally demanding problems: discriminating between overlapping reaction pathways and estimating reaction rate constants. Classical approaches, such as Sequential Monte Carlo ABC, rely on handcrafted summary statistics and scale poorly across large parameter spaces. To overcome this, we introduce a neural inference architecture. Our pipeline utilizes shared 1D-CNNs and a permutation-invariant Set Attention mechanism to learn low-dimensional summary statistics from variable-length, multi-experiment time-series data. These statistics simultaneously condition a model selection network to compute posterior model probabilities and model-specific conditional Invertible Neural Networks (cINNs) that estimate parameter posterior distributions. We demonstrate the practical utility of our approach on a real protodeboronation example, illustrating how it handles structural ambiguity and extracts kinetic parameters from competing catalyst deactivation pathways.

The Missile and the Toll: Two-Way Flow Through the Strait of Hormuz

Abstract
Tolls for safe passage and attacks on shipping are usually treated as mutually exclusive: either ships pay or attacks happen. We argue they are complementary. In a Stackelberg escalation game for the Strait of Hormuz, the enforcer chooses an attack intensity and a toll schedule; the response is to pay, cross exposed, wait, reroute, or exit. Attacks create the willingness to pay; tolls harvest it. The model predicts asymmetry across trade forms: a toll where the cargo owner can pay and the vessel owner will consent, and none where either fails. Any such payment is clandestine, but the model's implications are testable against freight rates, insurance premia, and incident reports.

The Geography of (Concurrent) Block Proposing
Authors: Ciamac C. Moallemi, Mallesh M. Pai, Dan Robinson, Christoph Schlegel
First: 2026-10-13T17:48:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:48:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The resilience and censorship resistance of a blockchain depend on where the validators and proposers of that blockchain physically operate. We study the economic incentives behind this location choice, both with a single proposer and with multiple concurrent proposers. Formally, we study a game where proposers first choose locations and then compete in a first-price auction for an opportunity whose common value is driven by information from geographically dispersed sources. A single proposer minimizes variance-weighted delay, locating at a weighted median of the sources and co-locating with any dominant one. By contrast, with two proposers, information both observe equally early earns no rent, and each proposer's expected profit is proportional to the standard deviation of the information it receives before its rival. We show that the location game admits the jointly unobserved variance as an ordinal potential, so global solutions of a weighted two-median problem are equilibria. With two sources, one proposer locates at each, however weak one is. With more proposers and at least as many sources, equilibrium locations are distinct under regular bidding. Multiple concurrent proposers thus do more than limit any single proposer's control over inclusion: by rewarding exclusive rather than shared information, they create an economic force toward geographic decentralization.

Abstract
We establish theoretically that transverse spin textures are generically induced by the spin-orbit interaction at the Fermi surface of centrosymmetric ferromagnetic metals. We confirm this prediction with {\em ab initio} computations for Fe, Co and Ni. By symmetry, this cannot be associated with spin-orbit torques under a uniform current. However, a new {\em current-gradient-induced} torque emerges in nanoscale geometries. For a series of Ni-based bilayers, the computed Ni thickness dependence of the current-gradient-induced torque is in quantitative agreement with measurements previously attributed to orbital torques. We discuss how this opens a new avenue for the electrical control of magnetic orders, possibly also including the Néel vector of metallic altermagnets.

Relative-Entropy Geometry and Convergence in Nonconvex Wiretap Optimization
Authors: Farzin Salek, Masahito Hayashi
First: 2026-10-13T17:35:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:35:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We develop and analyze an entropic descent method for a nonconvex optimization problem on probability simplices arising in classical and quantum secure communication. The optimization variables describe a randomized encoder, allowing its input distribution and stochastic preprocessing to be optimized jointly. An exact relative-entropy identity yields a channel-independent majorization bound and an explicit mirror-descent iteration with a fixed unit step size. We establish quantitative descent, convergence of objective values, and stationarity of accumulation points on their support faces for arbitrary finite classical channels, without assuming degradedness or less noisiness. For strictly positive transition matrices, a square-root parametrization and a Kurdyka--Łojasiewicz argument establish finite length of the transformed trajectory and convergence of the entire sequence, including limits on the simplex boundary. For less-noisy channels, a restricted convex formulation admits global convergence with an explicit \(O(1/t)\) objective-error bound. We extend the relative-entropy construction to noncommuting quantum outputs and derive monotone alternating updates for covariant encoders, including correlated preprocessing over a fixed block. Numerical experiments compare the method with analytic benchmarks and constrained optimization, reveal multiple attracting values, and demonstrate strict improvements from stochastic preprocessing.

We develop and analyze an entropic descent method for a nonconvex optimization problem on probability simplices arising in classical and quantum secure communication.
Deterministic and programmable generation of mechanical multipartite entangled states

First: 2026-10-13T17:32:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:32:44+00:00

Abstract
Multimode mechanical resonators offer a large set of densely packed, long-lived bosonic modes within a single compact device, making them attractive for quantum simulation, quantum information processing, and quantum-enhanced sensing. Many such applications rely on multipartite entanglement, yet deterministic mechanical entanglement has so far been largely limited to pairs of modes. Here we demonstrate the deterministic and programmable preparation of multipartite entangled states among the phonon modes of a high-overtone bulk acoustic wave resonator coupled to a superconducting transmon qubit. By sequentially applying optimal-control-engineered isometries between the qubit and successive modes, we generate matrix product states of bond dimension two, including $W$, Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ), and cluster states. We fully characterize these states via quantum state tomography and certify genuine multipartite entanglement among up to four modes. We further demonstrate correlated-noise sensing by exploiting the complementary decoherence responses of the stored $W$ and GHZ states, isolating inter-mode phase correlations from local decoherence and placing quantitative bounds on collective dephasing. Our results establish a scalable approach to preparing multipartite entangled states in mechanical systems, with promising applications in multiparameter sensing and tests of quantum mechanics with massive objects.

Abstract
Massive mechanical oscillators couple directly to forces, accelerations and gravity, making them attractive probes of new physics and of quantum mechanics at macroscopic scales. Pushing sensitivities beyond the standard quantum limit for signals of unknown phase requires non-Gaussian quantum states, whose metrological advantage has so far been confined to lighter systems such as trapped ions. Here we operate a microgram-scale acoustic mode of a high-overtone bulk acoustic wave resonator as a phase-insensitive quantum-enhanced sensor. Our results establish acoustic resonators prepared in non-Gaussian states as quantum-enhanced probes for high-frequency gravitational waves, dark matter and wave-function collapse.

Abstract

Abstract
Goal of this paper is to study classes of Cauchy-Dirichlet problems which include parabolic equations of the type $$u_t -Δu= a(x,t)f(u)\quad\hbox{in $Ω\times(0,T)$}$$ with $Ω\subset\mathbb{R}^N$ bounded, convex domain and $T\in(0,+\infty]$. Under suitable assumptions on $a$ and $f$, we show logarithmic or power concavity (in space, or in space-time) of the solution $u$; under some relaxed assumptions on $a$, we show moreover that $u$ enjoys concavity properties up to a controlled error. The results include relevant examples like the torsion $f(u)=1$, the Lane-Emden equation $f(u)=u^q$, $q\in(0,1)$, the eigenfunction $f(u)=u$, the logarithmic equation $f(u)=u\log(u^2)$, and the saturable nonlinearity $f(u)=\frac{u^2}{1+u}$. The logistic equation $f(x,u)=a(x)u-u^2$ can be treated as well. Some exact results give a different approach, as well as generalizations, to [Ishige-Salani2013, Ishige-Salani2016]. Moreover, some quantitative results are valid also in the elliptic framework $-Δu=a(x)f(u)$ and refine [Bucur-Squassina2019, Gallo-Squassina2024].

Abstract
Gauge-equivariant graph neural networks generate ordered products of gauge links through local message passing, but the geometry of these internal structures is not captured by the receptive field alone. We investigate the statistical ensemble of closed lattice walks generated by recursive node- and edge-feature operations, independently of network training. Analytical and numerical results reveal a separation between rapid growth of contour length and much slower spatial expansion: deeper compositions generate increasingly long paths through repeated traversal of relatively compact regions. These findings identify complementary roles of network depth in extending spatial reach and enriching the representations constructed within that reach, providing a geometrical perspective on learning lattice gauge observables.

Abstract
Financial analysis requires reasoning over heterogeneous signals, including company fundamentals from regulatory filings and trading signals derived from market prices. Abstract
A transformer trained on sequences whose source parameters are drawn afresh each time can learn only what the sequences share: the source family and its prior. The Bayesian predictor that knows both, which we call informed Bayes, is the unique minimizer of the training loss and is computable from the family alone; we use it as a quantitative, falsifiable reference. Where earlier work compared transformers with Bayes on sources whose contexts can be learned independently, we build Markov sources with up to 64 symbols in which observations in one context inform prediction in another: product sources with known or hidden encodings, transition rows sharing an unknown base law and depth, and a mixture of three families. Our main result is that informed Bayes predicts, from the family and prior alone, how a trained transformer behaves in four tests that go beyond the learning curve: the loss that remains under a source outside the training family (predicted 2.237 bits, measured 2.309), the doubling of the extra loss when two independent components change rather than one (0.345 predicted, 0.350 measured), the benefit of other contexts when a token appears for the first time, and the depth at which copying an earlier continuation starts to pay. On the learning curves themselves, small transformers match informed Bayes when the shared structure is visible in the tokens, and hidden structure needs more layers and training, with gaps below 0.01 bits per token for the largest models. We explain why the imitation is possible and where it must fail: informed Bayes decomposes into sufficient statistics that attention selects and accumulates and an estimator, fixed by the prior, that the feed-forward network learns; training pays the complexity of informed Bayes under the architecture's prior, and finite-memory universal prediction bounds how closely a bounded network can reach it.

Abstract

Abstract
We introduce PULSUS, a direct frequency-domain framework for multidimensional spectroscopy of open quantum systems beyond the impulsive rotating-wave approximation. Coherence-frequency intervals are represented through Liouvillian resolvents, while finite Gaussian pulses are incorporated through operator-valued functions of the field-free Liouvillian. This enables direct evaluation on Cartesian grids or arbitrary spectral coordinates while retaining molecular evolution during the pulse envelopes and, when required, the complete light--matter interaction. The implementation is validated against analytical and independent time-domain benchmarks and supports targeted spectral sampling that reduces the number of evaluated coordinates when the response is spectrally localized. A controlled hierarchy of finite-pulse, rotating-wave, short-pulse, and impulsive models separates the errors introduced by each approximation. For a coupled electronic dimer, the total impulsive-RWA error is nonmonotonic in pulse duration: an intermediate regime provides the highest accuracy, whereas ultrashort pulses expose coupling-enabled beyond-RWA pathways and longer pulses develop substantial finite-pulse corrections. These results show that the impulsive and rotating-wave approximations cannot be assumed to improve simultaneously and should be tested when quantitatively interpreting multidimensional spectra. PULSUS provides a direct frequency-domain framework for carrying out that comparison.

Abstract
Photonic reservoir computing is a promising physical machine-learning approach for time-series prediction. Quantization of the reservoir response signal is an important consideration in the implementation of photonic reservoir computing, and the number of quantization bits and sampling frequency need to be optimized to achieve high prediction performance and low energy consumption.
