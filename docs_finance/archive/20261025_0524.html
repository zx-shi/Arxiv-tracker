Phase Transition from Random Features to Fully Trainable Networks
Authors: Xin He, Huan Lao, Xiliang Lu, Shijun Zhang
First: 2026-10-22T17:59:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:59:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Constructing accurate and computationally tractable approximation spaces is a fundamental task in scientific computing. Partially trainable neural networks bridge fixed random features and fully adaptive representations. This paper quantifies a phase transition pattern in approximation bounds and observes analogous behavior in training errors: a rapid but smooth initial improvement followed by diminishing returns, with a sharper transition under greater initialization mismatch. Under polynomial spectral decay with exponent $α>1$ and leverage compatibility, we establish a squared $L_2$ approximation rate $\mathcal{O}((κ_P\log(m/δ)/m)^α)$ for two-layer networks of width $m$ and trainable proportion $P$, where $κ_P=(P/κ_1^*+(1-P)/κ_0^*)^{-1}$. The bound holds uniformly over the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) with probability at least $1-δ$. The constants $κ_0^*$ and $κ_1^*$ quantify the fixed and trainable groups' sampling mismatches relative to the target RKHS. For spherical biased-ReLU features in dimension $d$, we verify these assumptions with $α=(d+2)/(d-1)$ and $κ_0^*=4\sqrt{2}τ(d+1)/d$ for initialization scale $τ\ge1$, and $κ_1^*=1$ under ridge leverage sampling. For $d=5$ and $τ=10$, a trainable proportion of approximately $4.1\%$ captures $90\%$ of the reduction in the approximation bound from $P=0$ to $P=1$. A structured three-layer example illustrates how a few trainable first-layer neurons exploit fixed downstream blocks through nonlinear composition. Numerical experiments across diverse settings, including networks with up to 15 layers, exhibit similar phase transitions in training errors.

Magnetic Activity as a Driver of Low-gravity-like Spectral Morphology in Late-M Dwarfs

Abstract
We present a systematic investigation of the connection between magnetic activity and the low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphology of late-M dwarfs. Our sample includes TRAPPIST-1 observed with JWST NIRISS/SOSS and 22 late-M dwarfs observed with IRTF/SpeX, together with flare observations from K2, TESS, JWST NIRISS/SOSS, and literature-reported X-ray luminosities. Using one-component PHOENIX/NewEra spectral modeling, we find that all targets are better reproduced by lower-surface-gravity model spectra than by models with the expected high gravities of field late-M dwarfs. The retrieved surface gravities moderately correlate with independently measured gravity-sensitive spectral indices, with the strongest trends seen for FeH$_z$ at 0.998~$μ$m and FeH$_J$ at 1.2~$μ$m, both showing statistically significant Pearson and Spearman correlations with $p<0.005$. They also show strong anti-correlations with flare luminosity and X-ray luminosity: for both relations, Pearson and Spearman coefficients are $<-0.9$, with $p \leq 1.0\times10^{-4}$, indicating that more active stars tend to have spectra favoring lower-gravity morphologies. Time-series spectra of TRAPPIST-1 during two large flares show no clear short-timescale variations in selected photospheric indices, suggesting that persistent small-scale magnetic activity may play a more important role than large flares in modifying the photospheric temperature--pressure structure. A simplified radiative-transfer experiment further shows that activity-associated heating can produce lower-gravity-like spectral features even when the input surface gravity is fixed. Our results suggest that underestimated surface gravities in late-M dwarfs may reflect atmospheres modified by frequent, small-scale magnetic activity, such as microflares, producing emergent spectra that mimic lower-gravity morphology.

WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory Alignment in Video Diffusion Models
Authors: Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Tong Zhao, Ruibo Li, Joey Tianyi Zhou, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00

Abstract
Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering. However, their use in spatial tasks is limited by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsistencies, and entangled camera motion with scene dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, generalize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose WorldForge V2 to achieve accurate camera control while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework using warped references to iteratively refine generation along a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts from imperfect references. Together, these components guide viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an additional model evaluation. We further extend motion localization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This enables reliable camera control with very little training data, with a single training scene already supporting generalization to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled video generation.

Abstract
Using CPS data for 1976 to 2025, we explore how wage inequality has evolved for married couples with both spouses working full time full year, and its impact on household income inequality. We also investigate how marriage sorting patterns have changed over this period. To determine the factors driving income inequality we estimate a model explaining the joint distribution of couples' wages that accounts for spouses' employment decisions. This requires a multivariate distribution regression model that incorporates a double selection rule. We find that income inequality has increased for these households and increased wage sorting has exacerbated the inequality resulting from individual wage growth. We find that positive sorting partially reflects the correlation across unobservables influencing the wages of both members of the marriage. We decompose the changes in sorting patterns over the 50-year sample into structural, composition and selection effects and find that the increase in positive sorting primarily reflects the increased skill premia for both observed and unobserved characteristics.

Quantitative constructive representation of the KdV flow in $H^3$ via $L^2$-energy-preserving centered-difference Chernoff approximations and Picard iteration
Authors: Comlan Edmond Koudjinan
First: 2026-10-22T17:49:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:49:50+00:00

Abstract
We give a constructive representation of the solution of the KdV equation $u_t=-u_{xxx}+6uu_x$ on $\mathbb R$ for real initial data in $H^3(\mathbb R)$. The approximations couple an exact, unitary centered-difference Chernoff product for the Airy group with a skew-symmetric discretization of the quadratic term, and are computed by Picard iteration; they preserve the $L^2$ norm exactly, as does the limit. A two-mesh Cauchy argument shows that the whole sequence converges in $C([0,T];H^2)$, at rate $O(n^{-\frac{2(s-2)}5})$ for $H^s$ data ($3\leq s\leq 7$), and in $L^2$ at rate $O(n^{-6/5})$ for $H^3$ data. This scheme, to the best of our knowledge, is novel and is of independent interest. As a by-product we obtain existence and uniqueness of strong solutions, Lipschitz dependence on the data in the $H^2$ norm, uniformly on bounded subsets of $H^3$ and compact time intervals, and joint locally Lipschitz dependence of $(t,f)\mapsto S(t)f$ into $L^2$; the classical conservation laws extend the flow globally in time.

Volatility-Corrected Prediction Intervals: Locally Adaptive Uncertainty Quantification for Probabilistic Time-Series Forecasting
Authors: Jason B. Cho, David S. Matteson
First: 2026-10-22T17:42:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:42:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Prediction intervals from probabilistic time-series forecasters are commonly assessed by marginal coverage, the long-run proportion of observations falling inside the intervals. However, forecast errors in time series are often serially dependent and heteroskedastic, so prediction intervals can attain near-nominal marginal coverage while miscovering systematically during periods of larger local error variance. We identify and address this failure mode, which we call \emph{local scale miscalibration}. We first document the phenomenon using time-series foundation models (TSFMs): across several real-world series, their native intervals often attain near-nominal marginal coverage while exhibiting substantial temporal variation in local coverage, alternating between overcoverage and undercoverage over shorter windows. We then propose Volatility-Corrected Prediction Intervals (VCPI), a post-hoc calibration procedure that uses the base forecaster's predictive scale as a baseline, models the volatility of standardized forecast residuals with a Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) model, and applies a multiplicative correction to produce locally adaptive prediction intervals. We justify VCPI both theoretically and empirically, comparing it against model-agnostic conformal counterparts. We provide a theoretical analysis of interval adaptation of the proposed method following abrupt changes in volatility. Empirically, we evaluate VCPI on Chronos, Moirai, and TimesFM across four real-world datasets and find that it produces more stable local coverage than conformal baselines while maintaining near-nominal marginal coverage and comparable interval width.

Authors: Viktor Stein, Levin Maier, Wuchen Li, Gabriele Steidl
First: 2026-10-22T17:32:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:32:11+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The attention mechanism underpins modern large language models, yet their dynamics remain only partially understood. Recent perspectives interpret attention blocks as interacting particle systems, whose mean-field limits correspond to gradient flows of energy functionals on spaces of probability densities on $\mathbb R^d$ equipped with Wasserstein-type metrics. We extend this viewpoint by deriving accelerated attention blocks from inertial, Nesterov-type damped Hamiltonian dynamics on these density spaces, developing the geometry and differential calculus needed to make the construction rigorous. In particular, we introduce a local-mobility geometry for the Otto-Wasserstein and softmax-Wasserstein settings, and a quotient Stein geometry that resolves the degeneracy of the linear-attention kernel to yield a well-defined metric and differential calculus. We provide rigorous foundations when softmax attention admits a gradient-flow formulation and when its inertial counterpart admits a damped Hamiltonian on explicitly characterized domains. For linear attention, we obtain a finite-dimensional solution theory with sharp conditions for global existence versus finite-time second-moment blow-up. We further prove a quantitative link between continuous moment blow-up and the discrete-layer depth at which token norms cross a prescribed threshold. Finally, motivated by the finite-token geometry, we propose geometric corrections to token and momentum updates. In decoder-only, nanoGPT-style experiments on TinyStories and OpenWebText, these corrections lower validation negative log-likelihood on next-token prediction relative to momentum-only controls.

Abstract

Abstract
India's reservation policy entitles designated groups to fixed fractions of faculty positions. Positions are indivisible, arrive over time across departments, and appointments are irreversible. We show that no rule can keep cumulative reserved counts nondecreasing while rounding entitlements to adjacent integers at both the department and university levels; university-level compliance forces department-level errors that grow without bound. This impossibility explains why both solutions practiced in India provoked litigation and protests. A random-roster solution preserves irreversibility and department-level compliance, matches entitlements in expectation, and concentrates university-level errors. Dropping cumulative accounting restores exact rounding at both levels.

Dynamics of random recurrent neural networks: colored noise and sublinear activations
Authors: German Mato, Facundo Rigatuso, Gonzalo Torroba

Abstract
We study the dynamics of recurrent neural networks with random couplings, sublinear odd activation functions, and temporally correlated noise. Using large N methods, we determine stationary correlations and distinguish the loss of local linear stability from the onset of chaos, characterized by a positive largest Lyapunov exponent. These thresholds delimit an intermediate regime in which local instability coexists with asymptotic stability under a common noise history. Their separation depends on both the noise correlation time and the asymptotic growth of the activation function. We show that internal noise with any finite correlation time cuts off the divergence of the network's intrinsic correlation time tau_corr at the edge of chaos; it exhibits a critical scaling tau_{corr,max} propto 1/sqrt{D_0}, where D_0 is the integrated noise strength. Static random input instead produces coincident thresholds and a divergent correlation time. We also calculate the frequency-dependent participation dimension from a four-point correlation function. Its zero-frequency value is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the network correlation time, predicting a smooth minimum at the weak-noise chaos threshold. Numerical simulations support the large-network predictions and reveal appreciable finite-size effects.

On the Token Collapse Phenomenon in Textual Graph Soft Prompting
Authors: Andrea Giuseppe Di Francesco, Rishabh Jain, Franco Maria Nardini, Nicola Tonellotto, Pietro Liò, Fabrizio Silvestri
First: 2026-10-22T17:13:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:13:03+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Recent methods for injecting graph structure into LLM prompts include graph soft prompting, in which a graph neural network (GNN) learns graph-specific tokens that capture relational information among entities in textual prompts. G-Retriever combines graph soft prompts with a verbalized description of the same graph to strengthen the textual prompt signal. We conduct an in-depth analysis of G-Retriever and uncover a pathological behavior we term token collapse. Across three benchmarks, three graph encoders, two LLM families, and both frozen and LoRA-adapted models, G-Retriever's graph tokens become almost indistinguishable across inputs. We provide evidence that the same failure is inherited by subsequent methods, even with multi-token pooling. Under the standard setup, swapping a token with the dataset mean, a token from another example, or one derived from a corrupted graph produces no resolved answer-level effect in accuracy. Moreover, a learned constant prompt that never accesses the graph matches or surpasses G-Retriever on all three benchmarks. These findings indicate that, rather than conveying graph content as intended, the collapsed token functions primarily as a generic soft prompt. We also examine conditions linked to this collapse and propose a two-stage procedure that yields non-collapsed tokens while maintaining competitive performance in most cases. However, token variability alone does not prove that the model uses graph information: via interventions and decoding probes, we show that depending on the configuration, a token can reflect the question, an answer prior, or graph-derived content that the LLM may or may not use. Overall, our results offer a practical approach to evaluating graph-token alignment rather than inferring it from task accuracy alone. Code to reproduce our experiments is available at https://github.com/difra100/Token_Collapse_KGQA.

Authors: Franco Terranova, Guillermo Bernardez, Albert Cabellos-Aparicio, Nina Miolane, Abdelkader Lahmadi

Abstract
Graph combinatorial optimization (GCO) has attracted growing interest, as many NP-hard problems naturally admit graph formulations, yet their combinatorial explosion renders exact methods computationally intractable. Recent advances in Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have significantly improved learning-based GCO solvers. However, existing approaches face limitations in both generalization across diverse graph instances and computational scalability as action spaces grow. To address both challenges, we introduce \textit{projection agents}, a GCO methodology built on the Wolpertinger framework and adapted to a shared GNN-based latent space for observations and actions. These agents are trained to solve GCO problems by predicting a desired latent action in a single forward pass, which is subsequently decoded into a valid discrete action in the graph using the latent space. Across diverse benchmarks, our approach achieves up to 6.41$\times$ faster inference and up to 75\% better generalization than existing solutions using a simple nearest-neighbor decoding, while opening the door to strong RL performance in super-linear decision spaces with multiple interdependent variables. Finally, we release \texttt{LaGCO-RL}, a Python library that automates latent action-space construction and supports existing RL-GCO solutions, promoting reproducibility and adaptation to new GCO benchmarks.

Abstract
Measuring the total mass of the Milky Way and nearby galaxy groups is difficult because classical dynamical estimators rely on assumptions about satellite orbital geometry that are rarely satisfied in practice, and because only a handful of satellite galaxies are typically available as kinematic tracers. We present a new framework that corrects the well-known Projected Mass Estimator (PME) using a residual neural network trained on thousands of simulated galaxy groups from the IllustrisTNG cosmological simulation. Separate networks are trained for each satellite sample size, from as few as 5 satellites up to 50, so that the correction automatically accounts for the statistical noise that dominates when only a small number of tracers is available. In tests on simulated halos, the classical PME systematically overestimates halo masses by factors of $M_{\rm proj}/M_{\rm true} = 1.30^{+0.72}_{-0.62}$ (using the 2D distance) and $1.46^{+0.97}_{-0.72}$ (using the 3D distance), with RMSE of 0.29 and 0.32 dex respectively. The neural-network correction reduces this to $M_{\rm proj}/M_{\rm true} = 1.02^{+0.30}_{-0.26}$ with an RMSE of 0.13 dex. Applied to the Milky Way, the method yields a total mass of $M_{\rm MW} = 1.144^{+0.399}_{-0.296}\times10^{12}\,M_\odot$, with estimates based on the brightest 5-10 satellites favoring a somewhat lower range of $(0.8-0.95)\times10^{12}\,M_\odot$. The modified PME gives a tighter constraint on the virial masses and the inferred dark matter content of galaxy groups and clusters.

Abstract
We developed a machine learning (ML) surrogate trained on instantaneous large eddy simulation (LES) data filtered using proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) to predict mean flow fields in natural rivers with complex geometries. The surrogate enables efficient prediction of mean flow fields downstream of marine hydrokinetic (MHK) turbine arrays at a fraction of the computational cost of high fidelity simulations, facilitating turbine layout optimization in natural waterways where bathymetry, turbulence, and turbine interactions influence the available energy resource. We demonstrate the capabilities of the model at a real world tidal river site under ebb tide conditions. Complex bed bathymetry and planform geometry generate substantial background turbulence that can obscure turbine wake signatures. POD is employed to extract relevant flow structures, improving the ability of the ML model to learn wake dynamics. First, LES is performed at the tidal site to generate training data. POD is then applied to decompose the instantaneous LES fields into spatial modes. Selected modes are used to train a three dimensional convolutional encoder decoder network to predict time averaged velocity fields. This selective filtering reduces the influence of dominant background fluctuations while preserving information relevant to turbine wakes. At equivalent network size and training cost, the POD filtered surrogate reduces the mean squared error by three orders of magnitude compared with the same network trained on unfiltered LES data.

Abstract
The sign problem poses a fundamental barrier to numerically exact quantum Monte Carlo simulations, severely hindering our understanding of quantum many-body physics across disciplines ranging from condensed matter to particle and nuclear physics. Geometrical frustration makes this barrier especially acute for interacting bosons. Here, we develop a bosonic auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (BAFQMC) method and rigorously prove that time-reversal symmetry and reflection positivity each provide a general sufficient condition for sign-problem-free simulations. Crucially, under either criterion, BAFQMC evades the sign problem even on frustrated lattices where conventional world-line and stochastic-series-expansion methods fail. Benchmarking our approach on a two-flavor Bose-Hubbard model on the triangular lattice yields excellent quantitative agreement with finite-temperature exact diagonalization. By establishing unifying principles to solve the sign problem in BAFQMC, our work substantially expands the reach of unbiased, numerically exact simulations for frustrated bosonic matter.

Abstract
We prove that Fekete configurations associated with a positive Hermitian line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold are asymptotically exact norming sets for random holomorphic sections. For every fixed $a>0$, the ratio of the supremum norm of a degree-$k$ section to its maximum on the degree-$\lceil ak\rceil$ Fekete configuration converges to one in probability. This holds for any background Kähler form: Fekete equidistribution provides enough sampling points near the maximum of the limiting pointwise variance. We obtain polynomial upper bounds for the tail of the ratio and almost-sure norm comparisons. In contrast, when the limiting variance is nonconstant, we construct separated arrays of cardinality comparable to the section-space dimension for which the ratio converges to a constant strictly greater than one. General separated arrays still satisfy probabilistic norming inequalities with degree-independent constants, and every such array is asymptotically exact if and only if the volume form is proportional to $c_1(L,h)^n$. We also prove a discrete $L^2$ norm comparison with exponentially small exceptional probability. The norm comparisons and convergence statements hold in both the Gaussian and spherical ensembles.

Causal inference in staggered adoption panels: The correct comparison set depends on your intervention

Abstract
A powerful data structure for causal inference is staggered adoption: many units are observed over many time periods, during which some units adopt an irreversible treatment at various time points while others remain untreated. Popular methods that apply in this setting include difference in difference, fixed effects, matching, and synthetic control. All of these methods compare the future outcomes of treatment adopters and non-adopters to answer the question: for units who adopt treatment, what outcomes would have been realized if they had remained untreated? We show that this question actually hides two very different causal questions of interest. The first is what would have happened under a momentary intervention to prevent treatment at its factual adoption time. The second is what would have happened to a treated unit under a longitudinal ban preventing treatment at all periods until outcome measurement. We show how to answer each question under conditionally ignorable treatment adoption or under parallel trends. We highlight that common methods cannot answer the second question because this question requires a time-varying comparison set with time-varying weights.

A powerful data structure for causal inference is staggered adoption: many units are observed over many time periods, during which some units adopt an irreversible treatment at various time points while others remain untreated.

Abstract
Critical heat flux (CHF) marks the onset of boiling crisis in light-water reactors, defining safe thermal-hydraulic operating limits. To support Phase II of the OECD/NEA AI/ML CHF benchmark, which introduces spatially varying power profiles, this work compiles and digitizes a broad CHF dataset covering both uniform and non-uniform axial heating conditions. We prove a Hörmander spectral multiplier theorem, i.e., the boundedness of sufficiently smooth multipliers of these Hardy operators on Lebesgue spaces. Abstract
We propose a new regression method named Direct Inference on Admissible Sets (Diona) that directly uses samples to estimate the state and its derivatives at a query point, along with their admissible bounds, without long global training. By casting the values and derivatives at the query and neighbouring samples as latent variables in a constrained optimisation problem, the burden of long training loops becomes one of on-demand local solves. We establish Diona's theoretical properties including finite-sample coverage and, in the noiseless case, convergence to the true state and derivatives under dense sampling. In a nonlinear regression benchmark on six functions of three to eight dimensions, Diona was more accurate than a Gaussian process (GP) on noiseless data especially with denser data sets. Furthermore, as with GP, it produced uncertainty bounds that contained the true value at more than 95% of query points in four functions. Diona's time to a first prediction after a data set update was three to four orders of magnitude shorter than that of the GP, and two to three orders shorter than that of a neural network, which was more accurate but offers no uncertainty quantification. This low latency is paid for by on-demand predictions, so this latency advantage applies when relatively few predictions are needed between data updates.

Abstract
We study equilibria in a closed, fee-free constant-function market maker (CFMM) economy with two assets and two agents. An interior state is a unilateral no-trade equilibrium exactly when the CFMM marginal price equals both agents' marginal rates of substitution. For an interior initial state, individually rational unilateral equilibria are Pareto optimal relative to the fixed CFMM invariant. A weak representative agent is obtained at each fixed equilibrium by weighted sup-convolution, whereas, under some regularity assumptions, a state-independent strong representative agent exists exactly when agents share the same homothetic preference. Every interior feasible state is reachable through finitely many valid trades, but an individually rational interior target need not be improvement reachable. Alternating utility-maximizing trades converge to a Pareto optimal unilateral equilibrium.

Abstract
We study Liouville-type theorems for stationary solutions of the incompressible magnetohydrodynamics equations in $\mathbb{R}^n$, with directional fractional dissipation $(-\partial_i^2)^αu$ and $(-\partial_j^2)^βb$, where $n\ge 2$ and $0<α,β\le 1$. We prove that $u\equiv b\equiv 0$ by assuming that $u\in L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $b\in L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, with suitable conditions on $p$ and $q$. The proof uses a localized fractional energy identity and does not assume finiteness of the global homogeneous Sobolev energy. The method also applies to the case of full fractional dissipation. For the system with common-directional fractional dissipation, we prove stationary uniqueness for compactly supported perturbations of arbitrary smooth transverse shear backgrounds when $0<α,β<1$. We also establish a quantitative uniqueness criterion for perturbations of backgrounds with bounded gradients that are not necessarily compactly supported, for all $0<α,β\le 1$, assuming directional localization.

Abstract
Discrete choice models are widely used in economics, transportation, and marketing to explain and predict how decision makers choose among a finite set of alternatives. The resulting choices depend on the socioeconomic characteristics of the decision makers, the attributes of the alternatives, and the conditions under which the decision is made. Because these factors interact in complex ways, and because real experiments raise confidentiality concerns, take time, and are costly, controlled experimentation with real respondents is often impractical. Simulation offers an alternative in which the data-generating process, and hence the true parameters, are known. This paper connects discrete choice modeling, experimental design, and simulation. Abstract
Pulsars are exceptional cosmic laboratories, the most stable of which rival atomic clocks. Nearly all of that precision rests on one observable, the average pulse profile unique to each pulsar and stable over decades. These profiles change shape systematically with observing frequency, reflecting the physics of the emitting pulsar beam, but if left unmodelled, it can bias the arrival-time measurements, undermining precision timing. Profile-domain timing has been proposed to address this effect, but current implementations use stochastic sampling, which is computationally prohibitive and has limited the application in large-scale analyses. Here, we present a simulation-based inference approach to profile-domain timing, using a normalising flow conditioned on a convolutional embedding of the frequency-phase portrait. From a single portrait, it returns joint posteriors on dispersion measures, arrival times, and the parameters governing profile evolution instantaneously, with uncertainties verified against known inputs following an initial training run. The value of a likelihood-free approach is that the simulator becomes the interface to pulsar properties. Fundamental properties like non-Gaussian noise, pulsar self-noise, jitter, scattering, and profile change can be inferred by simulating them rather than by deriving a new likelihood for each, opening a promising pathway to include machine learning methods into pulsar timing workflows.
