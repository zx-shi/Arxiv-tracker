Attention as Lattice Gauge Theory: Relations Interact Only Through Wilson Loops
Authors: Aakash Sarkar, Ananya Mukherjee, Motoshige Sato
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-27T17:58:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:58:48+00:00
Comments: 7 pages, 1 figure. Accepted at the 9th Workshop on Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences (ML4PS 2026), co-located with NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Relations compose through the entity they share - Alice is Bob's mother and Bob is Carol's father, so Alice is Carol's grandmother - and the order matters, since a father's mother is not a mother's father; verifying a composed relation means closing the loop. We show that if each entity's coordinate frame may be rotated independently (per-entity frame freedom), three things follow. In the pure-gauge sector, relations that share no cycle cannot interact at all. The only gauge-invariant score of a triangle is a class function of its holonomy $H = T_{ij} T_{jk} T_{ki}$ - a Wilson loop. And the only equivariant update of an edge is $U = Ψ(H)\, T_{ij}$ with $Ψ$ conjugation-equivariant. Edge attention by Wilson loops is therefore not one design among many but the unique form the symmetry permits. Three communities hold pieces of this form: the commuting-operator embeddings of knowledge graphs are its abelian (commuting) sector; AlphaFold's triangle update and the Edge Transformer have its shape - an edge updated through the third vertex - without its constraints; and lattice QCD builds it equivariantly on lattices. We instantiate the equivariant sub-family on general relational graphs as holonomy attention. With no training and composition alone, it infers unobserved $S_4$ relations on sparse graphs at 0.88 with a fifth of the edges corrupted, at or slightly below specialist synchronization algorithms, where every commutative architecture is capped at 0.083; its holonomy score flags corrupted triangles at AUC 0.99 where commuting scores are capped at 0.76 by a second selection rule, and it separates clusters without labels.

KPPax: preprocess differentiable kinetic ODE for machine learning in atmospheric chemistry
Authors: Wenqing Peng, Michael Boy, Zhi-Song Liu
First: 2026-10-27T17:58:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:58:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Atmospheric chemical mechanisms are commonly distributed in the language of the Kinetic PreProcessor (KPP), which generates efficient Fortran or C code but does not fit into the differentiable programming frameworks in which machine-learning models are developed. We present KPPax, a kinetic preprocessor that generates differentiable, just-in-time compiled, and vectorisable chemistry code in JAX from mechanisms written in the KPP language. KPPax evaluates the rate law with sparse operations in log space, which keeps it differentiable, and integrates the stiff system with the implicit solvers and the forward- and reverse-mode adjoints of the \texttt{diffrax} library, so that exact sensitivities are obtained without hand-derived adjoint code. We demonstrate two applications with the POLLU benchmark mechanism. In the first, the Fisher information matrix computed from an automatically differentiated Jacobian predicts, before any fit, which of the 25 rate coefficients a flow-tube-like design can identify, and noisy synthetic estimation confirms the predicted uncertainties of the nine identifiable rate coefficients to within about 30\% while revealing clusters of fast reactions of which only ratios are constrained. In the second, a neural-network correction of an emission inventory is trained offline on per-scenario 4D-Var retrievals and then fine-tuned online through the chemical model, which reduces the error of the biogenic correction by a factor of 2.4--5 and recovers information on low-temperature emissions that the individual retrievals lose. KPPax makes KPP mechanisms directly usable for parameter estimation and hybrid physics--machine-learning modelling.

A Tight 3-Approximation for Fair $k$-Means
Authors: Kangke Cheng, Guanlin Mo, Shihong Song, Hu Ding
First: 2026-09-07T20:54:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:56:47+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Fairness in clustering has attracted sustained research interest, motivated by the need to ensure equitable representation of protected groups in machine learning applications. We study fair $k$-means clustering in Euclidean space, where the proportion of each protected group in every cluster must lie within specified lower and upper bounds. These constraints make it challenging to determine both cluster centers and point assignments. Our algorithm first splits the input into many small fair clusters and then merges them into $k$ clusters with a weighted $k$-means algorithm. The merge is cheap only when the small clusters do not move points away from their optimal centers, so we add a displacement penalty to the split, which charges each point for how far the small clusters move it. Given a $ρ$-approximate algorithm for weighted $k$-means and any $ε>0$, our algorithm returns a fractional solution whose cost is at most $3ρ+ε$ times the optimal integral fair cost. With a PTAS as the subroutine, the approximation ratio becomes $3+O(ε)$, improving the previous factor of $5+O(ε)$. This ratio is the best possible: unless P=NP, no polynomial-time algorithm achieves the ratio $3-δ$ for any fixed $δ>0$, even in one dimension. The solution satisfies all fairness constraints exactly and can be rounded to an integral assignment with a bounded additive violation of fairness and no increase in cost. The same approximation guarantee extends to the $k$-sparse Wasserstein barycenter problem.

Quaternion Approximate Networks for Enhanced Image Classification and Oriented Object Detection
Authors: Bryce Grant, Peng Wang
Venue: IROS 2025 Oral
First: 2025-09-05T21:41:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:53:05+00:00

Abstract
This paper introduces Quaternion Approximate Networks (QUAN), a novel deep learning framework that leverages quaternion algebra for rotation equivariant image classification and object detection. Unlike conventional quaternion neural networks attempting to operate entirely in the quaternion domain, QUAN approximates quaternion convolution through Hamilton product decomposition using real-valued operations. This approach preserves geometric properties while enabling efficient implementation with custom CUDA kernels. We introduce Independent Quaternion Batch Normalization (IQBN) for training stability and extend quaternion operations to spatial attention mechanisms. QUAN is evaluated on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), object detection (COCO, DOTA), and robotic perception tasks. In classification tasks, QUAN achieves higher accuracy with fewer parameters and faster convergence compared to existing convolution and quaternion-based models. For objection detection, QUAN demonstrates improved parameter efficiency and rotation handling over standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) while establishing the SOTA for quaternion CNNs in this downstream task. These results highlight its potential for deployment in resource-constrained robotic systems requiring rotation-aware perception and application in other domains. Linear-quadratic mean field games with Volterra dynamics
Authors: Niccolò Fontana, Mattia Martini, Huyên Pham
First: 2026-10-27T17:48:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:48:54+00:00
Comments: 31 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study a class of linear-quadratic mean field games in which the state of each agent follows a stochastic Volterra equation of convolution type. The resulting dynamics are non-Markovian and account for memory effects through a completely monotone kernel, while the mean field interaction enters both the drift and diffusion coefficients. Exploiting a Markovian lifting of the Volterra dynamics, which restores the Markov property at the price of moving to an infinite-dimensional state space, we characterize the optimal response of a representative agent for a given population mean through a system of Riccati equations in Banach spaces, thereby obtaining a feedback representation of the optimal control. We then study the consistency condition defining the mean field equilibrium, which reduces to a forward-backward fixed point equation for the equilibrium mean: we prove existence and uniqueness of the equilibrium on short horizons and, when the control does not act on the diffusion coefficient, on arbitrary horizons under a monotonicity condition on the interaction. Finally, we consider the corresponding finite-player game and establish a quantitative propagation of chaos result. As a consequence, the feedback control of any mean field equilibrium, when implemented by each agent on its own state, yields an $\varepsilon_N$-Nash equilibrium for the $N$-player game, with an explicit rate as the number of agents tends to infinity.

Objectivity, basis ambiguity, and the classical simulability of concordant quantum computation
Authors: Bishal Kumar Das, Sourav Manna, Vaibhav Madhok
First: 2025-10-03T17:57:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:48:47+00:00
Comments: 21 pages (main text + references), 22 pages total including appendix; 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Concordant quantum computations, in which circuits whose register carries zero quantum discord with respect to every subsystem at every step, contain neither entanglement nor any other form of quantum correlation, yet no efficient classical simulation of them is known beyond restricted gate sets. We argue that the obstruction is not a shortage of correlations but the absence of a uniquely identifiable local product basis, and we develop this \emph{basis ambiguity} on two fronts. Quantitatively, we introduce the Basis Ambiguity Measure $β\in[0,1]$, the normalized dimension of the manifold of admissible diagonalizing bases on a gate support, and prove a sharp equivalence: a concordance-preserving gate that defeats the Local Basis Finder of Cable and Browne exists if and only if $β>0$. Both directions are constructive. Physically, we identify the resource whose absence the obstruction is: \emph{basis objectivity}, a redundant and independently accessible record of the local basis. We show that an environment in spectrum broadcast form, together with the system--environment interaction map, replaces the NP-hard degeneracy test of Eastin by a fixed number of pairwise fragment comparisons per gate, at a cost independent of register size and circuit depth; the two ingredients live on different axes (state and dynamics), and neither alone suffices. We further prove that a theory in which every state has a strictly unique decomposition into perfectly distinguishable pure states has a simplex state space and lacks the continuity content of Hardy's fifth axiom, so that $β$ is a computational witness of that axiom.

Optimizing Spectral Signature in Code Backdoor Detection

First: 2025-10-15T18:18:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:45:08+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the IEEE International Conference on Software Maintenance and Evolution (ICSME 2026). 12 pages, 4 figures

Abstract
As Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into software development workflows, they also become prime targets for adversarial attacks. Among these, backdoor attacks are a significant threat, allowing attackers to manipulate model outputs through hidden triggers embedded in training data. Detecting such backdoors remains a challenge, and one promising approach is the use of Spectral Signature defense methods that identify poisoned data by analyzing feature representations through eigenvectors. While some prior works have explored Spectral Signatures for backdoor detection in neural networks, recent studies suggest that these methods may not be optimally effective for code models. In this paper, we revisit the applicability of Spectral Signature-based defenses in the context of backdoor attacks on code models. We systematically evaluate their effectiveness under various attack scenarios and defense configurations, analyzing their strengths and limitations. We found that the widely used setting of Spectral Signature in code backdoor detection is often suboptimal. Hence, we explored the impact of different settings of the key factors. We discovered a new proxy metric that can more accurately estimate the actual performance of Spectral Signature without model retraining after the defense. Our findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the configurations of Spectral Signature defenses in securing code models and offer insights into potential improvements for future research.

A dichotomy for minimal submanifolds
Authors: Tobias Holck Colding, William P. Minicozzi
First: 2026-07-31T15:05:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:44:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We survey a circle of recent results showing that a minimal submanifold obeys a dichotomy: either it fills up space, spreading out like a space-filling curve, or it is confined, and confinement forces quantitative restrictions. On the confined side these restrictions are both geometric and function-theoretic: Euclidean volume growth, an optimal rate of convergence of the density, complex-curve rigidity for stable surfaces in $\bf{R}^4$, and a Liouville theorem forcing slowly growing harmonic functions on minimal disks to be constant. A prototypical restriction is Euclidean volume growth, which in these results is forced by the geometry rather than assumed. The mechanism is a volume doubling theorem that converts geometric confinement into quantitative rigidity: a stationary integral varifold trapped in a thin slab at a given scale cannot double its volume by more than a universal factor. We explain how this principle, and the height-excess bounds behind it, produce Euclidean volume growth for submanifolds of sublinearly growing height in every dimension and codimension, the optimal density rate in a slab, the complex-curve and Liouville rigidity above, a higher-codimension Bernstein theorem for disks, one-sided volume bounds, and an optimal stable Bernstein theorem in all dimensions generalizing Moser, Bombieri-De Giorgi-Miranda, Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck and Ecker-Huisken. We describe three regimes with sharp borderlines. In narrow cones the volume grows polynomially with exponent $n+O(\varepsilon^2)$, and the order $\varepsilon^2$ is attained; in wide cones the growth can be arbitrarily fast. For holomorphic curves in $\mathbb{C}^2$, there are also three regimes and the borderline is determined by the Kähler angle.

Working Memory with Polychronous Chains
Authors: Laurent U Perrinet
First: 2026-04-15T17:12:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:38:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Working memory --- the ability to store and recall precise temporal patterns of activity --- remains an open challenge for spiking neural networks (SNNs). A recurrent SNN of $N=1024$ neurons is proposed, in which each synapse is equipped with $D = 41$ heterogeneous delays, parameterised as a weight tensor $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times D}$ and trained end-to-end with surrogate-gradient backpropagation through time. Each stored motif is represented as a sequential chain of overlapping Spiking Motifs: contiguous context windows of length $D$ that uniquely predict the activity at the next time step. A closed-form Hebbian initialisation, derived by deconvolving the LIF membrane response and targeting a membrane potential value above the firing threshold $\vartheta$, already achieves high $F_1$ scores before any gradient step on a benchmark of $M=16$ motifs of duration $T=1000$ steps. With learning, the network reaches a mean $F_1$ score of $0.80$ over the motifs, and retrieval degrades gracefully when the trigger window is corrupted, with an $F_1$ score of $0.53$ at $25\%$ bit-flip noise. The network shows attractor-like retrieval dynamics consistent with hippocampal motif completion and with low-dimensional collective dynamics of population activity on manifolds. These results demonstrate that heterogeneous synaptic delays are an efficient and scalable substrate for working memory in SNNs, with direct implications for neuromorphic edge deployment.

A unified coarse-grained neural network potential for the dynamics of biomolecular complexes
Authors: Antonio Mirarchi, Stephen E. Farr, Adrià Peréz, Gianni De Fabritiis
First: 2026-10-27T17:37:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:37:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The behavior of biomolecular complexes is governed by chemically specific interactions and often involves rare events that atomistic molecular dynamics struggle to capture within accessible timescales. Here we introduce AMARO v2.0, a coarse-grained neural network potential that retains all heavy atoms and treats solvent implicitly. Hydrogen-aware bead types and total-charge conditioning provide a shared representation for proteins, drug-like molecules, and their complexes, including ternary protein-protein-ligand systems. The model preserves folded proteins and captures key features of protein conformational free-energy landscapes. Across 262 complexes spanning 11 targets, the learned potential retains most ligand poses, although quaternary structure reorientation is observed for the HIF2A ternary complex. In targeted case studies, AMARO v2.0 distinguishes the two protonation states of trypsin-benzamidine and reaches the crystal binding pose in 3.3-fold more trajectories than all-atom molecular mechanics at a fixed estimated GPU-time budget. The same framework also recovers a reference binding pose in TYK2 and supports binding free-energy estimation for both trypsin-benzamidine and TYK2-EJM31, although the resulting values are more energetically favorable than the experimental references. SHP2 and CDK8 systems expose remaining limitations in binding-pose recovery and site specificity, respectively. Together, these results support AMARO v2.0 as a shared heavy-atom potential for biomolecular complex dynamics.

What Prediction-Market Contracts Reveal at Launch: Trading Volume and Dispute Risk
Authors: Ahmed Mahrous, Roberto Di Pietro
First: 2026-10-21T11:20:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:36:58+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the 7th ACM International Conference on AI in Finance (ICAIF 2026). 8 pages, 5 figures. Code and data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23376383
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Using a near-census of 802,863 resolved markets listed on Polymarket between January 2023 and May 2026, and only information available at listing (the market's title and description, named resolution source, and contract metadata), we predict lifetime trading volume and whether settlement is later disputed. Contract information predicts trading volume well (out-of-sample $R^2 = 0.747$ for log volume), and most of the signal is interpretable: market topic provides the largest gain ($+0.289$ $R^2$ over a $0.220$ contract-feature baseline), wording and resolution-rule design add smaller but measurable improvements ($+0.027$ combined), and neural text models add little beyond the interpretable features. This performance does not survive a period of platform change coinciding with a shift in listing formats: accuracy in predicting whether a market reaches \$1,000 falls from AUC $0.940$ under same-period evaluation to $0.577$ when training on past quarters and testing on later ones. The loss concentrates in markets whose question forms were absent from training (76.9% of test markets; on familiar forms the model still reaches $0.778$), and a decomposition attributes more of it to a changed relationship between contract characteristics and demand than to an outdated mapping from text to features. Dispute predictors are more stable but less precise: launch-time features rank dispute risk far better than chance (AUC $0.78$), yet at a 0.31% base rate precision is too low for case-by-case prediction (average precision $0.012$), so the scores suit ranking groups of markets rather than forecasting individual disputes.

The Acridine Polymorph Landscape: Characterization and Selective Control by Additives
Authors: Adam Lahouari, Hengyu Zhou, Nikolaos Galanakis, Michael Kilgour, Daisuke Kuroshima, Jihye Han, Bart Kahr, Mark E. Tuckerman, Alexander G. Shtukenberg, Jutta Rogal
First: 2026-10-27T17:31:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:31:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Polymorphism in molecular crystals presents a major challenge for controlling crystallization. Here, we combine experiment and theory to characterize the polymorph landscape of acridine and investigate its selective control by additives. Melt crystallization experiments resolve the previously unknown structure of form VIII, while topological crystal structure prediction recovers all seven known unsolvated polymorphs with determined crystal structures. Combining a fine-tuned machine-learned interatomic potential with anharmonic free energy calculations reproduces the experimentally observed stability relationships and reveals a shallow free energy landscape. Finally, experimentally observed additive-induced polymorph preferences correlate with mixed-crystal substitutional defect energies and topological similarities between additive and acridine crystal structures. Together, these results provide a comprehensive picture of acridine polymorphism, linking crystal structure, thermodynamic stability, and additive-controlled crystallization.

A self-supervised machine learning approach to identify instrumental systematics in 21cm Cosmology
Authors: Ajith Sampath
First: 2026-10-27T17:29:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:29:58+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The primordial sound waves in the baryon-photon plasma are imprinted on the large-scale galaxy distribution as Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO), serving as a cosmological standard ruler to constrain dark energy and the Hubble parameter. The upcoming 21cm intensity-mapping arrays are designed to map the 21cm emission across large volumes and measure the BAO scale. However, radio interferometers suffer from instrumental systematics that can hinder detection of the cosmological 21cm signal by leaking bright foregrounds into the signal-dominated modes. This work aims to demonstrate that a self-supervised model can predict the instrument systematic state (here, the amplitude of a spectral ripple in the beam width) in interferometric visibilities and potentially disentangle the instrument from the sky without a forward model. Based on the fact that an instrument stays the same as the sky rotates in a drift-scan observation, and that the same sky is observed under different instrument states, we train a convolutional autoencoder to compress the information in the simulated visibilities and separate this information into instrument-only and sky-only latent vectors. The result shows excellent prediction of the unknown instrument systematic for 15 held-out instrument states with $R^2 = 0.9975$ with a linear probe, against $R^2 = -3.55$ for a random-encoder baseline. The instrument-latent carries no detectable sky signal, as measured with a total-power sky proxy, for any held-out test data. The sky-latent vector and instrument-latent vector show partial separation but not complete disentanglement, identifying sky-latent purity as the primary open problem. The results show the ability of the model to recognize the systematic and show a promising path towards an ML-based foreground cleaning pipeline. Full instrument-sky disentanglement constitutes the natural next step.

Abstract
