Variational wavefunctions for ideal anyon gas: superfluid and skyrmion Hall crystal
Authors: Ya-Hui Zhang
First: 2026-10-29T17:59:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:59:59+00:00
Comments: 31 pages, 10 figures (2 in the main text), including Supplemental Material
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The ground state of an ideal gas of anyons with statistical angle $θ\in(0,π)$ is a long-standing open problem, with renewed interest from the recent observations of fractional Chern insulators. In this work we construct variational wavefunctions that build in the exact anyon statistics and combine theory-guided composite-boson, composite-fermion and parton wavefunctions with neural-network correlations on the sphere. For spinless and SU(2) semions we confirm the paired anyon superfluid, well below the best crystal states. At $θ=π/3$ liquids and crystals are closer in energy for the spinless case. For $θ=π/3$ anyons with SU(3) flavor, we find two candidates. One is a skyrmion Hall crystal, in which the anyons form an integer quantum Hall state in the effective magnetic field from the spin texture. The other is a chiral color superconductor of six-anyon clusters, the color-flavor-locked pairing descendant of a holon metal with SU(3) color gauge symmetry and SU(3) flavor symmetry; its wavefunction is a hyperdeterminant rather than a product of parton determinants. Within our variational ansatz the color superconductor has better energy than both crystals and the color-flavor locked phases with U(1)$^2$ and Z$_3$ gauge groups. For twisted MoTe$_2$ this suggests that the reentrant integer quantum anomalous Hall phase is the skyrmion Hall crystal of the doped anyons, and that the observed superconductivity may be the color superconductor, which can undergo a transition to an $f+if$ BCS superconductor with a jump of the chiral central charge by 3.

Reconstructing hidden observables via quantum snapshot thermometry
Authors: Jie Ren, Andrew Hallam, Luheng Zhao, Huanqian Loh, Zlatko Papić
First: 2026-10-29T17:59:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:59:38+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Projective snapshots are a standard readout tool for quantum simulators, but they directly probe only observables that are diagonal in the measurement basis. We show how local equilibration and an independently calibrated equilibrium equation of state can reveal otherwise hidden conserved-density profiles. We demonstrate numerically that such ``quantum snapshot thermometry'' can recover energy fronts and a diffusion constant, distinguish spin transport from energy spreading, and reveal conserved-charge profiles invisible in the initial measurements. We also identify symmetry-imposed ambiguities in the readout and show how prior knowledge of the spatial structure can resolve them. Fixed-basis snapshot thermometry thus provides quantitative hydrodynamic information beyond transport exponents, bypassing additional measurement bases and quantum state tomography.

When Does Storage Complement Solar? Investment and Cost Recovery under Advanced Net Metering
Authors: Nathan Engelman Lado, Ahmed Alahmed, Audun Botterud, Saurabh Amin
First: 2026-10-29T17:59:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:59:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We examine the joint PV and storage operational and investment decisions of a prosumer under net energy metering (NEM) and peak/off-peak tariffs. Legacy NEM compensates net exports at the retail rate over a netting period, typically under time-invariant pricing. To better reflect system costs, tariff features are being reconsidered, with many U.S. states adopting time-varying prices and export compensation below the retail price. We formulate the prosumer decision problem as a cost minimization problem with charge/discharge, PV investment, and storage investment decisions. We analytically derive the optimal decisions based on the tariff's membership in one of six regions that partition the tariff space. Using these solutions, we examine the relationship between PV and storage, identifying when they are complements or substitutes. Furthermore, we identify the relationship between private DER incentives and the distribution utility's (DU's) net cost of serving the prosumer. For both storage and PV, when investment costs are greater than the costs of serving the customer, there is no tariff where investment in PV and storage decrease non-adopter bills. Under the same conditions, complementarity between PV and storage decreases DU cost recovery for a given tariff. In a Massachusetts-based case study, we validate our theoretical findings by connecting tariff design features to DER interaction, the DU's net cost, and DER adoption in a stochastic, multi-prosumer, and multi-period setting.

Abstract
The classic duck-rabbit illusion reveals a fundamental property of perception: when visual evidence is ambiguous, a visual system must resolve competing interpretations. But do humans and machine classifiers resolve the same ambiguity in the same way? To explore this, we use semantically ambiguous images as probes of human-machine perceptual alignment. Our psychophysically-informed framework interpolates between concepts in vision-language embedding space to generate continuous spectra of ambiguous images, enabling direct comparison of human and machine perceptual decision boundaries and sensitivities. We find systematic differences in these boundaries: machine classifiers are shifted relative to both human observers and the midpoint of the synthesis continuum. Guidance scale affects both bias and sensitivity in humans and the machine classifiers tested. Controlled semantic ambiguity in generated images, combined with a psychophysical framework, can thus enable quantitative evaluation of human-machine perceptual alignment.

SymAnt: Grounding Long-Horizon Action Anticipation with Adaptable Symbolic Priors

Abstract
As robots and intelligent systems increasingly interact with humans, they must anticipate long sequences of future actions from only brief observations. While purely neural models typically require extensive observations to predict future actions and remain difficult to interpret and adjust, symbolic priors offer a structured alternative. To this end, we introduce SymAnt, a novel neuro-symbolic approach that unifies the expressiveness of neural networks with the structured reasoning of scene and knowledge graphs. Given a short video, SymAnt performs a joint-search over both graphs to construct a symbolic context, enabling unified reasoning over spatial relationships, object attributes, affordances, and associated actions. This symbolic context serves as a prior for an iterative refinement process that progressively generates and improves the sequence of future actions. By grounding predictions in symbolic context, SymAnt anticipates long-horizon actions from short observation windows in a parameter-efficient manner, while enabling users to directly edit the underlying knowledge to adjust predictions without further training. Through our experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness of SymAnt, particularly when only short observation contexts are available, across five diverse datasets: Breakfast, 50 Salads, EPIC-KITCHENS, EGTEA Gaze+, and Assembly101.

Abstract
We study equilibrium computation in multi-unit trading networks with valuations satisfying generalized full substitutes and agent- and transaction-specific piecewise-linear transfer functions. These transfers model platform fees, taxes, subsidies, and membership discounts in online marketplaces. Our ascending procedure raises prices at different rates and reaches the minimum equilibrium price vector in finitely many iterations, each implementable in strongly polynomial time under our oracle and arithmetic models. An equilibrium allocation can then be recovered efficiently. The method extends primal-dual techniques developed for ascending auctions to handle complementarities between purchases and sales through a submodular flow formulation. In single-seller markets with substitutes and complements, our framework also extends the double-track auctions of Sun and Yang (2009) and Shioura and Yang (2015) to bidder- and good-specific transfers.

From signaling to interviews in random matching markets
Authors: Maxwell Allman, Itai Ashlagi, Amin Saberi, Sophie H. Yu
First: 2025-01-24T01:08:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:56:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In two-sided labor markets, interviews precede matches. Rising interview volumes in medical residency markets have motivated signaling mechanisms that let applicants express interest in a limited number of partners. We study how signaling reduces interviews in centralized random matching markets with post-interview shocks. We focus on interim stability, which ensures that agents do not regret not interviewing with each other. A matching is almost interim stable if it becomes interim stable after removing a vanishing fraction of agents. We consider markets with $n$ agents in which agents on the short side, long side, or both sides signal their top $d$ preferred partners. An interview occurs whenever at least one party signals the other. When the probability of a nonnegative interview shock is bounded away from zero and $d=ω(1)$, short-side signaling leads to almost interim stable matchings. Long-side signaling can fail in strongly imbalanced markets when pre-interview scores outweigh interview shocks. In balanced markets without interview shocks, both-side signaling fails to achieve almost interim stability when $d=o(\log n)$. For larger $d \ge Ω(\log^2 n)$, under the same probability condition, short-side signaling achieves perfect interim stability, while long-side signaling can fail in imbalanced markets. We extend these findings to propose a signaling mechanism for multi-tiered random markets and identify conditions for incentive compatibility. A technical contribution is the analysis of a message-passing algorithm for stable matchings on trees, together with local availability certificates used to study interim stability on general graphs.

In two-sided labor markets, interviews precede matches.
Field-level inference under pressure: the information content of the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich field

Abstract
The thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich (tSZ) field is highly non-Gaussian and contains cosmological information that cannot be fully captured by its angular power spectrum. In this work, we investigate the information content of this field using a convolutional neural network (CNN), and compare to simple and interpretable summary statistics, in a simulation-based inference (SBI) framework. We perform the first field-level analysis of the tSZ field, using a suite of $10{,}000$ simulated Compton-$y$ maps, where we jointly vary the cosmological parameters $Ω_c$ and $σ_8$ (the density fraction for cold dark matter and the matter clustering amplitude, respectively) and two parameters describing the amplitude and shape of the electron pressure profile, $P_0$ and $β$, that aim to capture astrophysical uncertainties. We consider both noiseless simulations and simulations including realistic Simons Observatory (SO) noise after component separation. We use SBI to compare the constraints obtained from the CNN with those from the three Minkowski functionals (MFs), individually and in combination, as well as the angular power spectrum. This is the first application of SBI to the MFs of the tSZ field. We find that the CNN provides tighter constraints than conventional summary statistics in the noiseless case. However, at SO-like noise levels, the combination of MFs and the power spectrum recovers constraints comparable to those obtained with the CNN. Our results highlight the potential of these interpretable summary statistics, as well as field-level inference, for extracting information from the tSZ field, motivating efforts toward their application to real data.

Abstract
A metric space admits an almost bi-Lipschitz embedding into a Euclidean space if and only if it is almost homogeneous. We prove the sufficient direction, complementing the known necessary condition. More quantitatively, we bound the target dimension and the embedding constants for every logarithmic exponent $γ>1$. The bounds depend only on the covering parameters in the almost homogeneity condition. In particular every doubling metric space admits such an embedding. This answers a question of Margaris and Robinson and the almost bi-Lipschitz part of a question of Olson and Robinson. As a consequence, the difference set of the image is doubling, which answers the difference-set questions of Olson and Robinson and of Margaris.

Optimal Policy With Noisy Sufficient Statistics
Authors: José Luis Montiel Olea, Mikkel Plagborg-Møller, Christian K. Wolf
First: 2026-10-29T17:45:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:45:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study how policy-makers should set $k$ policy instruments so as to best hit $m$ (perhaps dynamic) targets, in a linear-quadratic model where there is statistical uncertainty about the causal effects of the policies. Plugging the estimated effects into "sufficient statistics" optimal policy formulas, as commonly done in the literature, can result in infinite risk. In fact, maintaining the status quo regardless of the data is uniquely minimax-optimal when $m>1$, and uniquely minimax-regret-optimal when $m,k>1$. Nevertheless, we recommend that policy-makers adopt a simple, dampened version of the popular plug-in rule, as in Fisher (1962) and Brainard (1967). Not only does this cautious rule outperform doing nothing over most of the parameter space, its worst-case risk is also close to the minimax value in empirically relevant models. Policy-makers should use causal effect estimates from an unbiased but dispersed estimator (such as a local projection) rather than from a moderately biased but precise estimator (such as a vector autoregression).

When Imputation Changes the Nearest Neighbor: Exact Certification of Similarity Retrieval for Incomplete Sensor Time Series
Authors: Jianru Shen
First: 2026-10-29T17:45:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:45:34+00:00
Comments: Accepted at IEEE BigData
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Sensor time series routinely reach similarity search with missing values, and the retrieved neighbours then depend on how the gaps were filled. We ask which retrieval conclusions hold for every admissible completion of an incomplete query. For a family of window-minimum distances that contains the $L_1$ distance as its separable end we give a pairwise certificate that one candidate is strictly closer than another, a guaranteed candidate set that contains every possible nearest neighbour, and a certificate that a nearest-neighbour label cannot change. The guarantees hold uniformly over the specified box and apply to the true query whenever it lies inside. At the separable end a closed form is known, but beyond it the worst-case gap need not be attained at a corner of the box. We prove a finite reduction to critical values, give an exact interval dynamic programme for the worst-case ranking gap, and a quadratic-time fast layer of valid bounds and feasible witnesses, supported by an exact criterion for when the feature-box relaxation is tight. On three traffic-sensor collections with real gap patterns, linear interpolation changes the nearest neighbour in 15 to 33 percent of the queries. At the full band the feature-box relaxation misses up to 14 percent of the exactly stable boundary pairs, whereas fixed-completion and Monte Carlo checks issue false certificates. The fast layer resolves about 90 percent of all tested boundary pairs without dynamic programming, and on paired network-scale queries the exact method is 82 to 110 times faster than the programme alone. Label certificates transfer to three public benchmarks, and the band width trades forecasting accuracy and certification coverage for lower imputation sensitivity, with dataset-dependent crossovers.

"Deep lifts": Neural networks can generalize even with the dumbest optimizer
Authors: Sunil Madhow, Yu-Xiang Wang
First: 2026-10-16T06:37:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:44:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Given a labeled dataset $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i = 1}^n$, consider drawing a parameter vector $\hatθ$ uniformly at random from those that interpolate the data within a width-$W$ depth-$D$ ReLU architecture with continuous weights bounded within $[-M, M]$. We study the generalization properties of $\hatθ$ in the case where the dataset can, in fact, be explained by a smaller $w$-by-$d$ ReLU network. Despite the obliviousness of the selection rule for $\hatθ$, it adaptively generalizes at the rate $\tilde O\left(\frac{wdWD}{n}\right)$, rather than being governed by the VC dimension of the full $W$-by-$D$ hypothesis class. The reason is due to a particular inductive bias in ReLU networks: the $w$-by-$d$ parameter *witnesses* an exponential volume of $W$-by-$D$ interpolating parameters, which dwarfs the volume of poorly generalizing parameters (conditioned on interpolation). We explicitly construct this set of near-equivalent $W$-by-$D$ parameters by a proof technique called *deep lifting*, and then tie the achievable volume to stability properties of the $w$-by-$d$ witness. Examples show that these techniques allow us to convert approximation-theoretic results into generalization guarantees for nonparametric classes. We further show that generalization of uniform-random interpolators can survive label corruptions.

Abstract
For each prescribed $1<p<\infty$, we construct a compactly supported initial vorticity in $L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)\cap L^p(\mathbb{R}^2)$ that admits two distinct evolutions under the unforced two-dimensional incompressible Euler equation on $[0,1]$. At each finite cutoff, a prepared reference evolution remains close to rotating configurations, while a second evolution differs initially only at the finest scale. In the second evolution, the finest configuration consists of separating dipoles whose remote strain activates the instability of the next larger configuration. As the cutoff tends to infinity, the two families of initial data converge strongly, along a subsequence, to the same datum, while a nonzero difference survives at a fixed larger scale and is detected by a fixed smooth observable at a positive limiting time. Quantitative concentration and relative comparison estimates establish this cascade for exact Euler patch solutions. Strong compactness through inverse-flow stability then preserves both exact vorticity transport and the positive-time distinction between the two limiting evolutions. The initial datum and construction parameters may depend on $p$.

Abstract
We prove quantitative sub-ballisticity for the self-avoiding walk on the lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$, $d\geq 2$. Namely, we obtain an explicit exponential rate of decay for the probability that a self-avoiding walk of length $n$ reaches distance at least $vn$, in terms of $v>0$. Our approach is based on a quantitative analysis of the original nonconstructive proof of Duminil-Copin and Hammond using ideas from proof theory.

Abstract
Food proteins play a critical role as emulsifiers, where adsorption at the oil and water interface governs emulsion formation and stability. However, quantitative characterization of interfacial protein load remains challenging due to polydispersity in conventional emulsification methods. We combine microfluidic generation of monodisperse emulsions with colorimetric protein quantification to measure the post-collection initial interfacial load ($Γ_0$) and subsequently monitored over 48 h to obtain the saturation load ($Γ_\infty$). Interfacial load exhibited a strong bulk concentration-dependent trend and was well described by a pseudo-first-order model. Both $Γ_0$ and $Γ_\infty$ followed sublinear scaling with concentration ($Γ_0$ ~ C^{0.21}$, $Γ_\infty$ ~ C^{0.12}$). Interfacial loads were largely independent of droplet diameter, with modest reductions in initial loading for the smallest droplets at low protein concentration. Measured loads exceeded idealized monolayer predictions and fell between bilayer and trilayer bounds, consistent with accumulation beyond a simplified monolayer model. Additionally, high-throughput mapping was performed across three proteins, seven concentrations, three pH values, and two ionic strengths, revealing that $α$-Casein exhibited higher interfacial loads than $β$-Casein and Whey protein Isolate, with maximum adsorption near the isoelectric point and at elevated ionic strength. In summary, this study establishes a quantitative experimental framework for linking protein properties, formulation conditions, and interfacial behavior using monodisperse microfluidic emulsions, while requiring only milligram-scale protein quantities.

Abstract
Voice-cloning TTS models synthesize an unseen speaker's voice from brief speech prompts, but it remains unclear which acoustic properties they represent and use. We introduce a feature-based interpretability framework for autoregressive voice-cloning TTS models, analyzing features from sparse autoencoders (SAEs), PCA, and random directions via sparse probing, steering, and quantitative auto-interpretability. We focus on Qwen3-TTS and extend probing and steering to $5$ additional models to test generalization across architectures. These models encode concepts like pitch, energy, noise, and accent, but sparse prediction and steering are effective only for some concepts. Strong probing does not always translate to steering or vice-versa. SAEs are useful for sparse probing of human-labelled concepts and novel-concept discovery, while PCA is competitive for dense probing and steering. Auto-interpretability finds $684$ automatically validated SAE features in Qwen3-TTS covering speaker clusters, speaking style, and recording conditions beyond known concepts. Overall, we perform a comprehensive interpretability study of autoregressive voice-cloning TTS models.

Abstract
This paper presents a Lagrangian framework to solve Wasserstein Hamiltonian flows (WHF) under two-point boundary conditions on large finite graphs. Characterized by a coupled system of discrete master equation and Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) equation on the probability simplex, this formulation defines the macroscopic WHF. While classical density-based solvers can track this flow, they face severe memory and computational bottlenecks as the graph scale grows. To bypass these limitations, we introduce the stochastic Hamiltonian process (SHP), a sample-based mathematical framework that tracks individual particle dynamics rather than the full node-wise density. We parameterize the time-dependent potential using neural network, yielding the Neural SHP solver. Numerical experiments on synthetic graphs demonstrate the scalability of our method relative to density-based baselines. Furthermore, we validate Neural SHP across two large-scale real-world networks, a PubMed citation network and a massive road network, demonstrating its robust efficiency for graph partition and routing problems on complex graph structures.

Abstract
Quantum error correction is essential for fault-tolerant quantum computing. High-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes have emerged as a promising approach to reducing the substantial physical qubit overhead of quantum error correction, enabling encoding rates exceeding $1/2$ in blocks of approximately one thousand physical data qubits. However, their practical implementation requires decoders that combine high accuracy with low latency to keep pace with quantum hardware. Existing decoders of high-rate qLDPC codes can achieve high accuracy but often incur substantial computational latency, whereas neural network decoders have yet to be demonstrated for high-rate qLDPC codes. Here, we introduce \emph{neural triage decoding}, a framework that uses syndrome consistency to adaptively allocate neural computation. A local neural message-passing network rapidly predicts error corrections, additional iterations reduce residual syndrome violations, and a rarely invoked diversification stage searches for consistent corrections to the remaining shots. We demonstrate that the \texttt{Triage} decoder can decode ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes encoding a few hundred logical qubits, including lifted-product and Kasai codes, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy comparable to the near optimal algorithmic decoders while reducing decoding latency by approximately one to two orders of magnitude. Our framework is inspired by a rigorous analysis of the exact bitwise marginal decoder, revealing a distance-independent syndrome-inconsistent error floor. In addition, we show the robustness of neural network decoders to mismatched error priors. Our results establish adaptive neural decoding as a promising route towards fast, accurate, and scalable quantum error correction in the near term.

Abstract
For braneworlds with infinite extra dimensions, an analysis of the stability of the characteristic spectrum is essential for understanding their dynamical properties; in this study, we investigate the stability of the gravitational quasinormal-mode (QNM) spectrum of a thick de Sitter braneworld with a noncompact extra dimension, where the unperturbed background supports a localized graviton zero mode and a tower of purely imaginary QNMs. We first reconstruct the response of the warp factor and tensor effective potential to a smooth double-kink deformation of the bulk scalar field, and then introduce a superpotential deformation that parametrizes the resulting double-barrier structure; the deformed frequency-domain spectrum develops QNM pairs with nonzero real parts, and for the representative family studied here, a weak deformation can already generate a complex branch, although the least-damped pole remains purely imaginary in part of the parameter range. Abstract
When a resident community oscillates, can a rare mutant invade, and with which probability? We study a multitype birth-and-death process with interactions, in which population sizes are of the order of a carrying capacity $K$, and whose large-population limit, as $K\to\infty$, has a hyperbolically stable limit cycle for the $D-1$ resident types. We introduce a single individual of a new type. We prove that, as long as the mutant population is smaller than $\varepsilon K$ for a small $\varepsilon>0$, the resident population sizes divided by $K$ stay close to a moving point of the cycle, whose asymptotic phase moves by at most $O(\varepsilon)$ per period. We deduce that, for large $K$, the mutant dies out quickly if its growth rate averaged over one period, $f(\bar{\mathbf n})$, is negative, and that it invades with positive probability, in a time close to $\ln K/f(\bar{\mathbf n})$, if $f(\bar{\mathbf n})$ is positive. This justifies a criterion used heuristically in ecology for decades. The invasion probability, unlike $f(\bar{\mathbf n})$, depends on the phase of the cycle at which the mutant arises. We give it explicitly through Kendall's formula, and show that its average over the cycle, weighted by the birth rate of the mutant, equals $f(\bar{\mathbf n})$. In predator-prey, competitive and mutualistic examples, the invasion probability varies by a factor between $2$ and $600$ along the cycle, and individual-based simulations agree quantitatively with the theory. After invasion, the oscillations may vanish, persist on a cycle of higher dimension, or give way to chaotic coexistence.

Abstract
Yoga is widely practiced for improving physical and mental health, yet the biomechanical demands of individual yoga poses remain poorly characterized. Existing experimental studies typically examine only a small number of poses or muscles, limiting their ability to guide pose selection for rehabilitation or exercise prescription. Here we present a computational framework for predicting whole-body joint torques and contact forces from two-dimensional images of yoga poses. The framework combines manual pose digitization, a sagittal-plane MuJoCo human model, and constrained metabolic energy minimization subject to static equilibrium. Abstract
We develop a theory for estimating the kernel parameters of Bergomi-type stochastic volatility models from option-price time series. The framework allows positive kernels that may be singular at the origin, including the pure power-law kernel of the rough Bergomi model. Using the option-price representation of cumulative forward variance, we formulate the statistical experiment as exact observations of this quantity on increasingly fine time-maturity grids over a fixed horizon. The model has an infinite-dimensional state driven by a single Brownian motion, resulting in a rank-one leading covariance across maturities. Our construction takes maturity differences and then logarithmic time increments. Approximating the resulting diffusion coefficients by maturity-averaged kernels removes the unobserved forward variance curve from the leading term. Positivity of the kernel allows the leading covariance to be recovered from the marginal variances, motivating a weighted marginal composite likelihood contrast. We prove consistency without relative rate restrictions between the time and maturity meshes, allowing the true parameter to lie on the boundary. Under additional regularity and nondegeneracy assumptions and explicit mesh rate conditions, we establish $\mathscr F_1$-stable asymptotic normality, where $\mathscr F_1$ is the terminal model $σ$-field. The Gaussian limit has deterministic sandwich covariance and is independent of $\mathscr F_1$, so the limit is preserved under equivalent changes of probability measure. We also provide sufficient mesh rate conditions for the pure power-law kernel.

Abstract
We prove quantitative propagation of chaos in total variation for repulsive Vlasov--Riesz--Fokker--Planck (VRFP) equations on $\mathbb{T}^d$ and $\mathbb{R}^d$ with unregularized interaction potentials with singularity $|x|^{-s}$, $0<s<d/2$.
