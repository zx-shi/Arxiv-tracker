Global Multi-Maturity SPX-VIX Calibration Beyond Markovian Stitching
Authors: Atithi Acharya, Charlie Che, Yue Sun, Brandon Augustino, Shouvanik Chakrabarti, Shree Hari Sureshbabu
First: 2026-09-03T16:54:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We develop a global martingale optimal transport framework for joint S&P 500 (SPX)-VIX smile calibration across multiple maturities, without the conditional independence that Markovian stitching imposes between months. We prove that exact local and global feasibility are equivalent, yet the stitched laws, which carry earlier months only through the current SPX level, can form a strict subset of the global ones, so adjacent smiles cannot identify the dependence across months. We show that relative entropy against the standard Markov reference selects the stitched law, so this dependence must be supplied deliberately. We supply it through a finite-memory reference, in which a latent autoregressive state drives the VIX levels while every VIX marginal stays exact, and calibrate the resulting law on a junction tree whose size grows linearly with the number of maturities. On market data with up to nine SPX maturities, a law with memory and a law without it fit the same quotes but price VIX-conditioned SPX Asian claims differently: on every calendar, memory makes a claim on the maximum VIX cheaper and a claim on the minimum VIX dearer. Varying the memory strength on one calendar moves these differences steadily from zero, so the vanilla quotes, which fix the law of each date, do not determine prices that depend on the joint law across dates.

Accuracy of Uniform Inference on Fine Grid Points
Authors: Shunsuke Imai
First: 2025-12-21T07:39:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Uniform confidence bands are widely used in empirical analysis for uncertainty qualification of nonparametric inference of unknown functions. A variety of simple implementation methods, including multiplier bootstrap, have been proposed and theoretically justified. However, an implementation over a literally continuous index set is generally computationally infeasible, and practitioners therefore compute the critical value by evaluating the statistic on a finite evaluation grid. This paper quantifies the effect of this discretization on coverage accuracy and investigates how fine the evaluation grid must be to ensure valid uniform inference. We first illustrate through simulations of a kernel-based nonparametric estimator that coarse grids can invalidate uniform inference. Surprisingly, increasing the sample size can worsen coverage on a fixed coarse grid, even as the remaining approximation error on the grid decreases. We then derive general upper bounds on the coverage probability and coverage error of uniform confidence bands based on finite-grid multiplier-bootstrap critical values. These bounds distinguish discretization error from the remaining approximation error on the grid. For local empirical processes arising from kernel smoothing, we further establish primitive conditions under which coverage converges to zero, even when the number of grid points diverges, and conditions under which asymptotically nominal coverage is attained. These results yield rate-sharp grid resolution requirements in terms of the bandwidth.

Uniform confidence bands are widely used in empirical analysis for uncertainty qualification of nonparametric inference of unknown functions.
PathFM: Fast Generative Forecasting of Multivariate Trajectories
Authors: Jiachen Yao, Xiyuan Zhang, Annan Yu, Boran Han, Abdul Fatir Ansari, Shuai Zhang, Michael Bohlke-Schneider, Bernie Wang
First: 2026-10-15T17:58:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:58:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Time series foundation models are becoming increasingly popular for general-purpose forecasting. However, most leading models predict marginal quantiles, leaving the dependence structure of future trajectories unspecified. This distinction matters for trajectory-dependent quantities such as cumulative demand, peak load, and event timing, yet point and marginal scores do not reveal it. To model this dependence, we introduce PathFM, a trajectory-based forecasting model that uses flow matching to generate complete future paths. We further distill the model to one or two steps and share history attention cache across draws. Together, these enable PathFM to generate multiple paths for a single series in roughly the time needed for one query to a quantile model. The distilled sampler also makes it practical to optimize a calibration loss directly. PathFM achieves competitive marginal accuracy on the established forecasting benchmarks fev-bench and TIME. To complement these evaluations, we introduce Forecast Path Score Bench (fps-bench), which augments fev-bench tasks with energy and variogram scores and downstream metrics. PathFM achieves a higher aggregate path score on fps-bench than all evaluated external baselines. Experiments on chaotic systems further show that our flow model sustains long-run variability, while strong baselines rapidly collapse. PathFM makes generative time series forecasting both competitive and practical, enabling diverse downstream applications.

Time series foundation models are becoming increasingly popular for general-purpose forecasting.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are expanding from assisting scientific research and engineering to autonomously developing and improving AI systems, advancing AI for AI (AI4AI) as a pathway toward recursive self-improvement (RSI). Workflow-driven machine learning engineering (MLE) systems provide a practical path toward this pursuit through complex orchestration that manages long-horizon experimentation, while recent MLE systems adopt model-directed interaction, allowing models to autonomously organize experiments and adapt optimization strategies based on execution feedback. However, feedback from each experiment reflects several preceding decisions, and a long MLE run contains many such costly experiments. Training must therefore select useful supervision from long interaction trajectories and learn from intermediate outcomes. We propose a post-training method for autonomous long-horizon MLE optimization and apply it to train MLExpert, a 35B Mixture-of-Experts agentic model. We design the Kaggle-MLE Factory to construct executable tasks and an interaction harness for model-directed experimentation. Hierarchical agentic SFT transfers general tool-use and coding capabilities to MLE through outcome-guided supervision. Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL) treats the multi-turn decisions between successive experiment results as an execution window and assigns it a shared outcome-based reward. Asynchronous collection and policy correction support policy updates before the trajectory ends. On MLE-Bench Lite, MLExpert improves the average medal rate of the base model from 37.9% to 72.7%, achieving performance comparable to larger frontier models such as Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3. Further experiments show that MLExpert improves performance on NatureBench Lite, agent scaffold optimization, and algorithm optimization, suggesting that the learned optimization capability generalizes to other tasks.

Twice Upon a Time: Complementary Representations for Time-Series Reasoning
Authors: Hedi Zisling, Liran Nochumsohn, Ilan Naiman, Mihir Parmar, Omri Azencot
First: 2026-10-15T17:52:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:52:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Time Series Language Models (TSLMs) enable language-based understanding and reasoning over temporal data, yet a fundamental question remains: what information should their time-series representations preserve, and what variation should they factor out? Existing TSLMs typically expose the language model to a single representation, imposing a fixed inductive bias across downstream tasks. We instead introduce complementary representations as a design principle for time-series reasoning. Our model combines two visual views: line plots preserve absolute measurements and the observed coordinate frame, while normalized delay embeddings factor out positive amplitude rescaling and offsets while exposing temporal structure. Dedicated visual encoders integrate both views into a shared language model through visual tokens and multi-level feature fusion. Evaluation-time interventions verify that the two views exhibit distinct invariance profiles, while single-view ablations show that their combination is particularly beneficial beyond basic perception, with the large gains in prediction and reasoning. Built on Qwen3-VL-8B, our dual-view model improves overall TSRBench accuracy from 44.5% with line plots alone to 53.2%, substantially exceeding the strongest evaluated dedicated TSLM baseline at 36.7%. It further achieves 89.9% accuracy on TSExam and 86.8% numerical accuracy on multivariate ChatTS alignment. Together, these results demonstrate that complementary representations provide an effective design principle for general-purpose TSLMs, enabling models to exploit temporal structure without sacrificing access to the original measurement frame.

Learning the information contained in the microstructure of data distributions
Authors: Lucrezia L. Baldo, Chiara Lionello, Edward D. Donkor, Matteo Becchi, Giovanni M. Pavan
First: 2026-10-15T17:41:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:41:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding how much and where information is nested in sets of data obtained using different parameters and metrics is a typical problem in the analysis of high-dimensional datasets. Approaches based on relative data-driven metrics may be poorly quantitative and difficult to interpret, while accurate methods may be computationally expensive and impractical for large datasets. Here, we report an efficient, quantitative, and transferable method useful to this end, which we call "Micro-Information Analysis". Its foundation is based on the tight relationship that exists between the microscopic features of data distributions and the information they contain. The method performs preliminary unsupervised clustering, capturing microstructural features of data distributions such as the dominant Gaussian microdomains (microstates) present in them, their population, separation, and their residual non-Gaussian fraction. Leveraging the principle of clustering-and-minimization of Shannon entropy, this allows quantifying in bit (that is, in absolute metric) the information contained in the microstructure of the data, and how much information is nested in each Gaussian and non-Gaussian microdomain. The output of the analysis is a set of easily interpretable Information-Microstructure plots that allow to readily compare and rank data based on their information content and to graphically map the information breakdown among their various microenvironments in diagrams that are easy to interpret. We prove the efficiency of our method by analyzing and ranking data generated synthetically or from molecular simulations, demonstrating how "Micro-Information Analysis" is a physically-grounded quantitative tool useful for the study of physical systems from their data and for data science in general.

Replica Symmetry Breaking and Algorithmic Thresholds in Empirical Risk Minimization under Multi-Index Model
Authors: Andrea Montanari, Kangjie Zhou
First: 2026-06-26T19:57:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:27:20+00:00
Comments: 81 pages; 3 pdf figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern machine learning models are trained by optimizing high-dimensional non-convex empirical risk functions. Such cost functions can have a multitude of local optima and yet, gradient-based optimization appears to converge to near-global optima. Within a simple supervised learning setting, we develop a precise picture of which parts of the empirical risk landscape are accessible by polynomial-time algorithms. We are given i.i.d. pairs $\{(\boldsymbol{x}_i,y_i):\; 1 \le i\le n\}$ with $\boldsymbol{x}_i\in \mathbb{R}^d$ standard Gaussian feature vectors, and $y_i\in\mathbb{R}$ response variables that depend on $\boldsymbol{x}_i$ through their projections on an unknown $k$-dimensional subspace. We use empirical risk minimization to learn a model that depends on an $m$-dimensional projection of the data (e.g., an $m$-neurons neural network). We propose an incremental approximate message passing (IAMP) algorithm and precisely characterize the training error it achieves, as well as the relation between test and training error, in the high dimensional asymptotics $n,d\to\infty$, with $n/d\toα\in (0, +\infty)$. Based on earlier work in related models, we expect that the performance achieved by our algorithm is optimal among polynomial-time algorithms.

Modern machine learning models are trained by optimizing high-dimensional non-convex empirical risk functions.

Abstract
The human brain effortlessly deciphers colors and textures from visual stimuli and reconstructs the world around us, yet preserving such details remains challenging in image denoising. While significant progress has been made in generic image denoising, the problem of texture denoising has remained relatively under-explored. Fine details, such as intricate patterns and repetitive structures, often suffer from over-smoothing even in deep learning based approaches for denoising. In this paper, we analyze the behavior of state-of-the-art (SOTA) denoisers on texture datasets and show that performance improvements are possible only by fine-tuning the denoisers on the texture datasets. Since denoising quality directly impacts downstream tasks such as generative modelling, we argue that dedicated texture aware denoisers are essential for faithful texture reconstruction and synthesis.

Abstract
Transformers have achieved remarkable success across domains, motivating the rise of Graph Transformers (GTs) as attention-based architectures for graph-structured data. In this work, we study GTs through the lens of manifold limit models for graph sequences and establish a theoretical connection between GTs with GNN positional encodings and Manifold Neural Networks (MNNs). Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has significantly improved LLMs' mathematical problem-solving capabilities, but distilling such capabilities into smaller models remains challenging due to the capacity mismatch between verbose teachers and compact students. Directly copying teachers' lengthy reasoning chains causes capacity overload, resulting in truncated outputs or repetitive failure. Existing remedies each sacrifice a critical property of CoT: implicit reasoning methods (e.g., compressing reasoning into hidden states) trade away interpretability and verifiability, while heuristic compression strategies (e.g., random step pruning) destroy logical integrity. To address this, we propose BRIDGE, a curriculum framework that first establishes structural understanding via masked reconstruction, then uses GRPO-based reinforcement learning to guide students in self-discovering the optimal balance between accuracy and brevity, and finally internalizes complex reasoning through teacher-guided rewriting on failure cases. On GSM8K, BRIDGE enables Qwen2.5-3B to achieve 11.29% accuracy improvement and 27.4% token reduction over the original model, outperforming instruction-tuned variants and distillation baselines. Zero-shot transfer experiments on SVAMP and MATH-500 further confirm the generalization of internalized reasoning. Abstract
CompFlowLab is an open-source Python environment that is capable of modeling different classes of compressible flow problems (including shocks, flames, and detonation waves) using a one-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes solver with multi-species transport and chemical-reaction models. It is designed specifically for the data-driven modeling community as a lightweight, accessible prototyping platform to test, develop, and evaluate new modeling methods on numerically and physically challenging compressible flow problems, especially those featuring shocks and chemical reactions. Specifically, CompFlowLab aims at (1) providing computationally efficient calculations on advection-dominanted problems that are well-recognized to be difficult for conventional data-driven modeling techniques, such as shocks, flames, and detonation waves, and more importantly (2) enabling rapid testing and prototyping of new data-driven models. The code serves three primary purposes: (1) generating high-fidelity full order model data for training of data-driven modeling, (2) providing a modular platform for implementing and testing novel data-driven algorithms (e.g., machine learning methods and reduced-order modeling techniques) on challenging physics, and (3) offering a standalone Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) solver with validated test cases that can also support numerical method development in the broader CFD community. By unifying these capabilities in a clean, extensible Python codebase, CompFlowLab lowers barriers to innovation at the intersection of model reduction and complex fluid dynamics.

Abstract
We give a quantitative planar form of a multiscale argument for Falconer's distance problem. For every $0<η<1$, we obtain a positive lower bound for the Lebesgue measure of the distance set of a compact subset of the unit ball supporting a probability measure with $ν(B(x,r))\le 2r^{1+η}$. The bound depends only on $η$. For a compact set of diameter $D$ carrying a probability measure with $μ(B(x,r))\le A(r/D)^{1+\varepsilon}$, localization gives $|ΔE|\ge c_{\varepsilon,p}DA^{-p}$ for every fixed $p>1/\varepsilon$. In particular, compact planar sets of Hausdorff dimension greater than one have distance sets of positive Lebesgue measure. The proof develops the multiscale method of the September 2026 manuscript of OpenAI, using one sequence of restricted distance measures for both the mass and Fourier estimates. A reconstruction lemma combines summable total-variation errors with summable squared Fourier norms. No endpoint or sharpness assertion is made.

Abstract
Randomized block coordinate descent (RBCD) is a popular optimization algorithm for many large-scale machine learning problems. The existing theoretical analysis of RBCD mainly focuses on its convergence behavior from an optimization perspective, while its generalization behavior remains less understood for nonconvex problems. In this paper, we establish high-probability generalization guarantees for RBCD with nonconvex objectives. In the general nonconvex setting, we derive bounds on the gradients of both empirical and population risks, requiring only standard smoothness assumptions. With polynomially decaying step sizes, we tune the iteration number to balance optimization and generalization, thereby quantifying how early stopping leads to the optimal convergence rate for the population gradients. In the gradient-dominated setting, we provide bounds in terms of functional suboptimality, which nearly match the optimistic rates in convex and low-noise settings. Finally, we develop dimension-independent bounds for loss functions of a specific structure, making the generalization guarantees applicable to high-dimensional problems.

Abstract
Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Learning Variable Metrics for Convergent Proximal Splitting Algorithms
Authors: Yodai Suzuki, Shunsuke Ono
First: 2026-10-15T16:53:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:53:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose learned variable metric (LVM), a framework that learns metrics of proximal splitting algorithms adapted to each problem instance, coordinate, and iteration with a convergence guarantee. Proximal splitting algorithms can solve a wide variety of convex optimization problems but often require many iterations to converge. Choosing a suitable metric is an effective remedy, and variable metric theory naturally extends convergence guarantees to metrics that vary over the iterations. Learning is a promising way to design such adaptive metrics beyond the reach of manual design, but a naive implementation leads to (i) an expensive evaluation of a network at every iteration and (ii) difficulty in satisfying the conditions that the theory imposes on the whole infinite sequence of metrics. To resolve these difficulties, we first train a neural network to generate a few representative metrics once per problem instance, and then obtain the metric of every iteration by combining them with basis functions of the normalized iteration index. Abstract
For more than a century, the lattice thermal conductivity of crystalline solids has been known to decrease with increasing temperature, as the phonons that carry heat scatter more strongly via Umklapp processes. Here we report a pronounced violation of this rule in the layered charge-density-wave (CDW) metal 2H-TaSe2. Such a high-temperature reversal, in which a crystal conducts heat more efficiently as it is heated, is rare in crystalline solids. Its thermal conductivity minimum lies deep in the disordered phase well above the ordering transition, unlike the weak, transition-centered anomalies of other charge-density-wave systems, pointing to heat conduction governed by fluctuating rather than established order. We also reveal that the charge order melts not abruptly but through a fluctuation- and defect-driven, weak first-order process, leaving short-range correlations far above the transition. This work establishes fluctuating charge order as a sensitive control knob for thermal transport in quantum materials and thermal conductivity as a powerful probe of the hidden charge order.

Abstract
