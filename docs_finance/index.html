XStack: A Benchmark for ML Repo Migration
Authors: Linrong Cai, Taiming Lu, Haozhe Chen, Zhuang Liu
First: 2026-11-05T18:55:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:55:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
ML code should run where researchers choose, not where it was first written. Today, machine learning code mainly lives in two ecosystems, PyTorch on NVIDIA GPUs and JAX on Google TPUs. Migrating repositories between them means building and testing across frameworks and hardware. This migration is (1) a practical need, since it unlocks different hardware for researchers, and (2) a natural benchmark: it involves different frameworks and hardware, covers whole repositories, and is long-horizon and challenging. We introduce XStack, a benchmark that evaluates agents on migrating ML repositories between these two ecosystems. Each agent receives a PyTorch repository and a clean TPU machine and must deliver a JAX repository. Its output passes if it meets our grading criteria, based on the source repository's published result, authenticated and calibrated by us. Migration by agents remains unreliable: Claude Opus 5 passes 75%, GPT-5.6-sol 45%, and every other agent at most 40%. XStack is a step toward freeing ML code from its ecosystem. We release XStack to make progress toward reliable migration across frameworks and hardware measurable.

An Accelerated Proximal Gradient Method for Bures-Wasserstein Space
Authors: Jakob Nylöf, Sloan Nietert, Julien Pallage, Giancarlo Ferrari-Trecate, Daniel Kuhn, John Lygeros
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-05T18:55:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:55:10+00:00
Comments: Submitted on September 2 to the Geometric Distributional Deep Learning workshop at NeurIPS 2026, accepted
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study composite optimization over the Bures--Wasserstein (BW) space of Gaussian measures. Our main observation is that a useful class of BW problems can be lifted into the Euclidean space of affine transport maps, such that the lifted problems can be solved efficiently using accelerated proximal gradient methods. For objectives whose smooth and nonsmooth components admit convex smooth and convex proximable affine lifts, respectively, our algorithm BW-FISTA achieves minimax-optimal objective gap $O(k^{-2})$ after $k$ iterations. We verify these regularity conditions for potential and interaction energies together with common spectral regularizers, and we treat stochastic gradients under a suitable noise model. As a principal application, we consider Gaussian variational inference with a log-concave, log-smooth target. Here, the resulting entropy term is handled by an efficient, exact proximal step, and we improve the previous rate of $O(k^{-1})$ to $O(k^{-2})$. We numerically verify this predicted boost with synthetic experiments.

Quantum Knowledge Distillation: State-of-the-art, Applications and Open Problems
Authors: Aldana Al-Sulaiti, Kuan-Cheng Chen, Muhammad Bilal Akram Dastagir, Ahmed Farouk, Saif Al-Kuwari
First: 2026-11-05T18:53:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:53:05+00:00
Comments: 21 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Knowledge distillation transfers predictions, representations, or other supervisory information between learning models and is increasingly investigated in quantum machine learning. Its applications extend beyond compression to cross-architecture transfer, circuit approximation, distributed learning, and classical deployment of models trained using quantum-derived supervision. This survey reviews these approaches using two complementary classification axes: teacher-student configuration and the role of quantum resources in the distillation process. Quantum-inspired methods are discussed separately from methods that simulate quantum computations classically or execute them on quantum hardware. We explain how measurement probabilities, expectation values, classical readouts, state overlaps, and circuit-level objectives support different transfer mechanisms. We then critically assess the presented results against baseline design, execution conditions, uncertainty, and resource costs. The evidence shows that distillation can improve selected quantum students, but benefits are task- and architecture-dependent, and several studies find negligible gains or even performance degradation. Evaluations on physical quantum hardware remain limited, with some testing individual quantum components rather than complete learning pipelines. Heterogeneous protocols and incomplete resource reporting prevent general conclusions about practical quantum advantage. We identify priorities for controlled benchmarking, noise-aware learning, reproducible evaluation, and efficient transfer between quantum and classical models.

Adversarial Training with Large Step Sizes: Implicit Bias, Optimization, and Sharpness
Authors: Yi Feng, Andrea Paudice
First: 2026-11-05T18:48:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:48:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Adversarial Training (AT) is an important method for improving model robustness. We study the dynamics of deterministic Gradient Descent (GD) in AT. Most existing works in this direction focus on the small or vanishing step sizes regime, where GD resembles a continuous-time flow. However, such step sizes can make training inefficient and are rarely used in practice. In this paper, we study the AT dynamics of GD with large constant step sizes. We first study GD in adversarial linear logistic regression, focusing on two aspects: where the model parameters converge (implicit bias) and how quickly the losses decrease (optimization). We then turn to neural networks training and study how the sharpness of losses evolve during AT. Our results advance the understanding of the behaviors of GD with large step sizes in AT and highlight three key differences from its behavior in Standard Training (ST): (i) the implicit bias gap between AT and ST, previously established only for small step sizes, persists for arbitrarily large constant step sizes, (ii) different from ST, there is no uniform rate of convergence for the optimization error in AT, and (iii) sharpness can exhibit two-stage dynamics that differ from the Edge-of-Stability behavior observed in ST.

ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts
Authors: Yubo Wang, Rui Meng, Jaehyun Nam, Tomas Pfister, Jinsung Yoon
First: 2026-11-05T18:45:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:45:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reproducing machine learning experiments requires reconstructing procedures from information scattered across papers, released code, and supporting artifacts. For coding agents, reproduction success can therefore depend on their ability to fill in missing details, rather than on the released artifacts alone. We ask whether preserving recovered procedures and decisions improves subsequent independent reproduction. We define a standardized artifact design comprising a refined codebase, environment setup, experiment launchers, and a reproduction guide, and introduce ReproBridge, an agent workflow for constructing these refined reproducible artifacts. ReproBridge-Bench provides 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments across 36 papers, anchored by five human-verified reference scores per task. Agents attempt tasks in fresh sessions with raw code or refined artifacts; a separate Auditor checks execution evidence. Across four evaluated models, refined artifacts raise mean reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% and reduce downstream API spending by 19.7% on average. The cross-model success-rate spread narrows from 21.6 to 3.9 percentage points, with all four models achieving mean success rates above 90%. Removing any component reduces success in the ablation study, with the largest losses from code and environment removal. These results suggest that preserving execution knowledge helps even models with lower raw-code success reproduce experiments more reliably and at lower API cost.

Deep-learning surrogate model for rapid quantum-noise computation in dual-pump fiber-optic phase-sensitive amplifiers
Authors: Yousra Bouasria, Debanuj Chatterjee, Abdessamad Belfakir, Debabrota Basu

Abstract
Fiber-optic parametric amplifiers in a phase-sensitive amplifier (PSA) configuration are well known for their low noise properties. Quantum mechanics dictates that these amplifiers can have a 0 dB noise figure (NF) (ratio of signal-to-noise ratio at the input and output of the amplifier) under certain conditions. However, for a dual-pump configuration, when the system is highly nonlinear, fully quantum calculations of NF become non-trivial. In such cases, semi-quantum models of NF evaluation exists, however, they are often complicated and resource intensive. To simplify that, in this work, first we use a semi-quantum NF calculation framework for the generation of numerically simulated input-output dataset of NF over a large parameter spaces. Then we use the data to train an optimized deep neural network (DNN) for rapid NF prediction. Utilizing the DNN, we demonstrate about 400 times reduction in the computation time compared to the standard semi-quantum NF evaluation approach with a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0015 dB. Using clustering we demonstrate a rapid exploration of the parameter space with respect to high gain (20 dB) and low NF (0.3 dB) targets and retrieve associated design parameters. These results illustrate the efficacy of machine learning-based approaches for rapid search of high-dimensional parameter spaces especially in compute intensive photonic systems.

DiffSequence-Q: Diffusion Design with Quantum Assistance for Biologically Active Proteins
Authors: Ke-Xin Tang, Zhen-Qiang Weng, Kai Yan, Yao-Yao Lu, Yu-Ze Zhu, Hang Song, Jun-Jie He, Jie-Cheng Yang, Xiang Zhao, Lin Yang, Xian-Min Jin
First: 2026-11-05T18:28:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:28:09+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Designing protein sequences that satisfy both secondary structure requirements and solubility targets remains challenging. We present DiffSequence-Q, a hybrid multinomial diffusion model conditioned on secondary structure at each residue and a continuous solubility target. To introduce quantum auxiliary supervision during training, we employ a quantum neural network (QNN) or quantum Kolmogorov-Arnold network (QKAN) as an auxiliary solubility prediction head over shared Transformer representations. The auxiliary prediction loss is jointly optimized with the diffusion objective, allowing quantum auxiliary supervision to influence the learned denoising model. Once trained, protein sequences are generated entirely through classical reverse diffusion. On the CATH benchmark, DiffSequence-Q achieved the highest secondary structure recovery among the four evaluated models for complete sequence generation. Evaluation with a separately trained solubility prediction model showed that the best QNN and QKAN configurations achieved mean predicted solubility scores 14.0% and 22.7% higher than the multilayer perceptron (MLP) baseline, respectively. Experimental characterization further demonstrated biological activity across three distinct protein families. Selected defensins exhibited pronounced inhibitory activity against Aspergillus niger, while xylanases and short-chain dehydrogenases exhibited substantial enzymatic activity. Together, these results support DiffSequence-Q as a framework for integrating structural requirements and solubility targets in protein generation, with experimentally demonstrated biological activity.

Cross-Border Ownership and Input Access: Evidence from the Lithium Supply Chain

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How does cross-border ownership shape the lithium supply chain? Using global data on mines and refineries, I estimate a model in which ownership can lower refiners' effective ore costs, encourage mines to restrict spot sales, and soften competition. To study these effects, I examine policies motivated by national security concerns about reliable access to critical mineral inputs: divestiture of Chinese stakes in overseas producers and a ban on ore exports to China. Divestiture increases spot ore supply but leaves lithium output nearly unchanged, while continued trade preserves Chinese access to ore. The export ban reduces global lithium supply because refining capacity elsewhere cannot fully replace lost Chinese production. Ownership transfers do not reserve ore for domestic production; restricting Chinese access imposes a substantial supply cost at existing capacities.

Implicit Regularization via Spectral Neural Networks and Non-linear Matrix Sensing
Authors: Hong T. M. Chu, Xiaoyu Dong, Subhro Ghosh, Chi Thanh Lam, Soumendu Sundar Mukherjee
First: 2024-02-27T15:28:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:23:13+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The phenomenon of implicit bias has attracted interest in recent years as a fundamental aspect of the remarkable generalizing ability of neural networks. In a nutshell, it entails that gradient descent dynamics in many neural nets, even without any explicit regularizer in the loss function, converges to the solution of a regularized learning problem. In this work, we show that this phenomenon extends to non-linear spectral parameterizations in matrix sensing. More precisely, we propose a matrix network architecture called Spectral Neural Networks (abbrv. SNN) where each neuron applies nonlinear activation to the singular values of the input matrix. Conceptually, this entails coordinatizing the space of matrices by their singular values and singular vectors, as opposed to by their entries, a potentially fruitful perspective for matrix learning. We prove the exponentially fast decay of squared loss during the training dynamics of an SNN parameterized matrix sensing model under gradient flow (which is a continuous approximation of the discrete gradient descent), and also that the training implicitly selects an approximate solution that minimizes nuclear norm. We also demonstrate the effectiveness of the SNN architecture in the context of matrix sensing by empirical investigations. We believe that the SNN architecture has the potential to be of wide applicability in a broad class of matrix learning scenarios.

Data-driven discovery of intrinsic Lie point symmetries of differential equation systems
Authors: Ilker Gurcan, Kadir Ustun, Ramazan Gokberk Cinbis
First: 2026-11-05T18:19:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:19:28+00:00
Comments: 63 pages, 10 figures. Code and data availability statements are included in the manuscript
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Discovering continuous symmetries directly from differential-equation data can reveal geometric structure for model reduction and scientific machine learning without symbolic determining equations or a closed-form representation of the governing system. We formulate intrinsic Lie point-symmetry discovery as an operator-theoretic problem on sampled jet-space data. Admissible generators are identified from a sampled determining operator and separated from redundant, solution-dependent, and geometrically trivial directions. The framework combines prolongation, sequential subspace restrictions, and a fixed nonlinear dictionary linear in trainable coefficients. A rank-stabilization preflight test diagnoses systems whose represented intrinsic symmetry algebra does not stabilize under dictionary refinement. On a controlled differential-equation benchmark with an analytically known 11-dimensional represented intrinsic symmetry algebra, the method recovers the complete represented generator set, reproduces its Lie-bracket structure to numerical precision, and approximates a known generator beyond the training domain. Here we show that explicit intrinsic continuous-symmetry generators can be recovered from data obtained from exact or approximate differential systems.

Abstract
Molecular representations such as fingerprints and learned embeddings play a central role in cheminformatics and small-molecule machine learning. They serve as the primary input for a diverse array of tasks, including clustering, dataset splitting, and virtual screening, yet their evaluation is often limited to downstream model performance on narrow benchmark tasks. We carried out a systematic investigation of the quality of molecular representations, finding that none of the state-of-the-art molecular embedding models capture graph structure, 3D shape, or ADMET endpoint similarity better than fingerprints or physicochemical descriptors. Our findings provide an insight into the current limitations of molecular representations, outlining future research directions for the development of more robust encodings of small molecules.

Conformal Prediction for Time-Dependent PDEs
Authors: Joshua Stiller, Annika Schneider, Eyke Hüllermeier

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Uncertainty quantification is crucial in scientific machine learning, where models inform safety-critical tasks such as flood forecasting, financial risk management, and thermal control in machines. Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees, but in time-dependent settings common to physics and engineering, these guarantees can break down, leading to systematic undercoverage. We study this problem in the context of surrogate models for time-dependent physical systems described by partial differential equations. We prove that in a function space setting, distributions at arbitrarily close times can be mutually singular, making exact coverage guarantees impossible. We further show that in discretized settings, the total variation distance between distributions often saturates exponentially fast with the grid resolution, mandating restrictions on the spatial discretization to ensure coverage. Finally, for certain settings, we show how to use principled weighted conformal prediction to obtain finite-sample coverage guarantees over growing time horizons.

Abstract
Spintronic synaptic devices based on magnetic random access memory (MRAM) are a rapidly evolving class within the analog neuromorphic architecture. However, obtaining the multi-weight or analog behavior desired for improved neural network inference accuracy in scaled MRAM remains a challenge. In this study, we demonstrate and tune up to eight distinct resistance levels in 300 mm wafer-scale magnetic tunnel junction artificial synapses driven by spin orbit torque. By varying the applied in-plane field, multi-weight resistance states emerge through domain wall nucleation and pinning. Overall, this work demonstrates a route towards multi-weight artificial synapse MRAM, establishing a path towards scalable neuromorphic systems based on spintronics.

Abstract
Designing healthcare strategies requires anticipating their clinical and operational consequences before implementation, a task that often relies on health system simulation. Such a simulation necessitates modeling how patient states evolve and generate care demands. However, conventional approaches typically tailor compact state transitions to a single policy question and omit history-dependent dynamics, thus requiring the redefinition of states and transitions when there is a new question that entails different clinical information. We propose to learn a patient world model from longitudinal EHRs using a neural network to provide a shared clinical representation of patient trajectories that can serve simulation questions across scenarios. Taking ICU operations as an example, we train the model on MIMIC-IV, freeze it, and couple its time-queryable predictions to shared ICU calendars, resources, and queues via scenario-specific mappings, so that one model renders admission demand, care tasks, and discharge proposals across simulations evaluating ICU bed capacity, care dispatch, and post-ICU transfer capacity. Experiments show that our model matches Markov and semi-Markov comparators in aggregate occupancy and directional responses to capacity and dispatch policies. It also reproduces policy gains from replayed observed trajectories. For patients entering the same support state from different prior trajectories, the learned representation captures a 24-h recovery difference (-6.4 predicted versus -6.7 observed percentage points), a contrast missed by compact state models, which propagates into distinct simulated care demand, queue burden, and bed use. These results indicate that a learned world model of patient dynamics has the potential to replace question-specific state models, allowing new care policies to be evaluated by reconfiguring the scenario without model rebuilding.

Abstract
The logarithmic vector multiplicative error model (log-vMEM) provides a methodology for modeling and forecasting multivariate positive-valued time series. However, the number of autoregressive parameters increases rapidly with the system dimension and lag order, posing challenges for estimation and model selection. This paper develops a regularized estimation framework for the log-vMEM with multivariate Gamma errors by incorporating hierarchical lag structures to induce sparsity and accommodate heterogeneous lag dynamics. We consider three hierarchical lag structures: componentwise, own-other, and elementwise, in combination with group lasso, group MCP, and group SCAD, adaptive group lasso penalties. The real data analysis is carried out on the realized volatility measures for Microsoft (NASDAQ:MSFT) which allows a comparison of the competing regularization approaches in an empirical financial setting. Together, these developments provide a computational framework for sparse multivariate volatility modeling with flexible lag structures and dependent Gamma errors.

Abstract
Different optimizers impose different geometries on parameter updates. Adaptive methods such as Adam and AdamW use coordinate-wise updates, while matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon utilize weight-matrix structure through orthogonalized updates. For Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), however, the effective geometry of its weight matrices is shaped not only by its learned channel-mixing weights, but also by the graph-propagated representations it acts on. Thus, even if an update is well normalized as a matrix, its effect on the layer output can be amplified when applied to graph-propagated features. To address this gap, we introduce GraphMuon, a graph-aware optimizer that adapts Muon's matrix updates to the covariance structure of graph-propagated layer inputs. GraphMuon performs orthogonalization in a metric defined by these propagated representations and normalizes step sizes according to the update's induced effect on the layer representation. We show that theoretically, vanilla Muon can suffer from graph-induced update amplification, while our GraphMuon can mitigate this effect. As such, our GraphMuon yields a graph-metric analogue of Muon's polar update step. Empirically, GraphMuon achieves $1.32\times$-$2.42\times$ validation-loss time-to-target speedups over AdamW on three large-scale OGB benchmarks. Across 15 dataset-architecture configurations spanning node classification, inductive graph classification, and graph regression, it achieves lower time-to-target in 13 cases, while maintaining competitive or superior predictive performance. Code is available at https://github.com/guy-hadad/GraphMuon.

Abstract
At the edge of stability, the iterates of gradient descent oscillate along the sharp directions of the Hessian. This poses a significant challenge for continuous-time models as the usual continuous-time approximation -- the gradient flow -- admits no oscillations. Several continuous-time models have been proposed to capture the behavior at the edge of stability, among them central flow and rod flow. Although both models can be interpreted as performing a coarse-graining in time, the connection between them remains poorly understood. Abstract
Anomaly detection in multivariate time series is a critical task across a wide range of real-world applications, where abnormal behaviour is rare, labels are unavailable, and the cost of a miss is high. The central challenge is learning a characterisation of normality precise enough to flag deviations.
